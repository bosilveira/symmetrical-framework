### 11.4 Mellin-Barnes Residue Series (Non-integer $r$, $x>c$)

For non-integer shape parameters $r$, the Stieltjes transform does not reduce to a finite number of elementary terms. Instead, we can represent it globally as a Mellin-Barnes contour integral, which evaluates exactly to a dual infinite series. When $x>c$, this series is determined by closing the integration contour in the right half of the complex plane.

For non-integer $r > 0$ and $x > c$:
$$
\boxed{\; S_{f}(-x;r,c) = \frac{\pi}{2rc}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{n+1}\left(\frac{c}{x}\right)^{n}\sec\left(\frac{(n-1)\pi}{2r}\right) + \frac{\pi}{c}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} \frac{(-1)^{k}}{\sin\bigl(\pi(1+r(2k+1))\bigr)}\left(\frac{c}{x}\right)^{1+r(2k+1)} \;}
$$

**Derivation:**

This elegant result relies on the Mellin-Parseval theorem, evaluating the integral by summing two distinct families of poles in the complex $s$-plane.

1.  **Set up the Mellin-Parseval Integral**:
    The Stieltjes transform evaluated at $-x$ is $S_{f}(-x) = \int_{0}^{\infty} f(t) \cdot \frac{1}{t+x} \,\mathrm dt$. 
    By the Mellin-Parseval theorem, this is equivalent to:
    $$
    S_{f}(-x) = \frac{1}{2\pi i} \int_{\sigma-i\infty}^{\sigma+i\infty} \mathcal M\{f\}(s) \, \mathcal M\left\{\frac{1}{t+x}\right\}(1-s) \,\mathrm ds
    $$

2.  **Substitute the Mellin Transforms**:
    *   From §5.1, $\mathcal M\{f\}(s) = \frac{\pi c^{s-1}}{2r} \sec\left(\frac{(s-1)\pi}{2r}\right)$.
    *   The standard transform of the Stieltjes kernel is $\mathcal M\left\{\frac{1}{t+x}\right\}(1-s) = x^{-s} \frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi(1-s))} = x^{-s} \frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi s)}$.
    
    The integrand becomes:
    $$
    I(s) = \frac{\pi^2}{2rc} \left(\frac{c}{x}\right)^s \frac{\sec\left(\frac{(s-1)\pi}{2r}\right)}{\sin(\pi s)}
    $$

3.  **Contour Closure and Pole Identification**:
    Because we are evaluating the case $x > c$, the factor $(c/x)^s$ decays exponentially as $\operatorname{Re}(s) \to +\infty$. This allows us to close the integration contour with an infinite semi-circle in the **right half-plane**. By Cauchy's Residue Theorem, the integral equals **negative** the sum of the enclosed residues (because closing to the right creates a clockwise contour).
    
    There are two independent sets of simple poles in the right half-plane ($s>0$):
    *   **Set 1 (from sine)**: $\sin(\pi s) = 0 \implies s = n$ for integers $n \ge 1$.
    *   **Set 2 (from secant)**: $\cos\left(\frac{(s-1)\pi}{2r}\right) = 0 \implies \frac{(s-1)\pi}{2r} = \frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2} \implies s = 1 + r(2k+1)$ for integers $k \ge 0$.

4.  **Evaluate Residues for Set 1 (Sine Poles)**:
    The residue of $1/\sin(\pi s)$ at $s=n$ is $(-1)^n/\pi$.
    The residue of the full integrand is:
    $$
    \operatorname{Res}_{s=n} I(s) = \frac{\pi^2}{2rc} \left(\frac{c}{x}\right)^n \sec\left(\frac{(n-1)\pi}{2r}\right) \frac{(-1)^n}{\pi} = \frac{\pi}{2rc} (-1)^n \left(\frac{c}{x}\right)^n \sec\left(\frac{(n-1)\pi}{2r}\right)
    $$
    Applying the negative sign from the contour orientation converts $(-1)^n$ to $(-1)^{n+1}$, yielding exactly the **first sum** in the boxed formula.

5.  **Evaluate Residues for Set 2 (Secant Poles)**:
    Let $g(s) = \cos\left(\frac{(s-1)\pi}{2r}\right)$. Its derivative is $g'(s) = -\frac{\pi}{2r}\sin\left(\frac{(s-1)\pi}{2r}\right)$.
    Evaluated at $s_k = 1 + r(2k+1)$, the sine term is $\sin(\frac{(2k+1)\pi}{2}) = (-1)^k$.
    The residue of the secant term is $1/g'(s_k) = -\frac{2r}{\pi(-1)^k} = \frac{2r}{\pi}(-1)^{k+1}$.
    The residue of the full integrand is:
    $$
    \operatorname{Res}_{s=s_k} I(s) = \frac{\pi^2}{2rc} \left(\frac{c}{x}\right)^{s_k} \frac{1}{\sin(\pi s_k)} \frac{2r}{\pi}(-1)^{k+1} = \frac{\pi}{c} (-1)^{k+1} \frac{1}{\sin(\pi s_k)} \left(\frac{c}{x}\right)^{s_k}
    $$
    Applying the negative sign from the contour orientation converts $(-1)^{k+1}$ back to $(-1)^k$, yielding exactly the **second sum** in the boxed formula.

*(Note: When $r$ is an integer, these two sets of poles collide, leading to poles of order 2. The residues must then be evaluated using derivatives, which precisely recovers the logarithmic terms seen in the classical $r=1$ case in §11.2).*

4.  **Mellin-Barnes Dualities (§11.4):** The Stieltjes transform acts as an analytic prism. Via Mellin-Parseval, it takes the single secant form of the Mellin transform and shatters it across the complex plane, rendering the exact real-space result as a beautiful dual series governed strictly by the two competing families of poles.
