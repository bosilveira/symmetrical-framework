### 11.3 Large-x Asymptotic Behavior

When we evaluate the Stieltjes transform far out on the negative real axis (as $x \to \infty$), the transform exhibits a simple, universal inverse-distance decay. The coefficient of this decay is governed entirely by the total probability mass of the function.

As $x \to \infty$, the asymptotic behavior is:
$$
\boxed{\; S_{f}(-x;r,c) \sim \frac{\pi}{2rx} \;}
$$

**Derivation:**

The derivation relies on factoring the integrand and applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem to take the limit inside the integral.

1.  **Factor the Integral**:
    Start with the definition of the Stieltjes transform at $z = -x$:
    $$
    S_{f}(-x) = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(t;r,c)}{t+x}\,\mathrm dt
    $$
    Factor out $1/x$ from the denominator:
    $$
    S_{f}(-x) = \frac{1}{x} \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(t;r,c)}{1 + t/x}\,\mathrm dt
    $$

2.  **Evaluate the Limit ($x \to \infty$)**:
    To find the leading-order asymptotic behavior, we examine the integral as $x$ grows arbitrarily large. 
    For any fixed $t > 0$, as $x \to \infty$, the ratio $t/x \to 0$. Therefore, the denominator $(1 + t/x) \to 1$.
    Because $f(t;r,c) > 0$ and $1/(1+t/x) \le 1$ for all $x, t > 0$, the integrand is strictly bounded by $f(t;r,c)$. Since $f(t)$ is integrable, the Dominated Convergence Theorem allows us to pass the limit inside the integral:
    $$
    \lim_{x \to \infty} \left[ x S_f(-x) \right] = \int_{0}^{\infty} \lim_{x \to \infty} \left( \frac{f(t;r,c)}{1 + t/x} \right) \mathrm dt = \int_{0}^{\infty} f(t;r,c) \,\mathrm dt
    $$

3.  **Apply the Total Mass**:
    From §3.3, we know that the total integrated mass of the function over the positive half-line is a fundamental constant:
    $$
    \int_{0}^{\infty} f(t;r,c) \,\mathrm dt = \frac{\pi}{2r}
    $$
    Substituting this back yields the asymptotic limit:
    $$
    x S_f(-x) \to \frac{\pi}{2r} \quad \implies \quad S_f(-x) \sim \frac{\pi}{2rx}
    $$

**Physical/Statistical Interpretation:**
In physics contexts (where the Stieltjes transform represents an electrostatic or gravitational potential generated by a 1D charge/mass distribution), this result simply states that from very far away, the extended distribution $f(t)$ "looks" exactly like a single point particle located at the origin with a total mass of $\pi/(2r)$. 

For the normalized probability density $\rho = \frac{2r}{\pi}f$, this asymptotic limit perfectly normalizes to $S_\rho(-x) \sim \frac{1}{x}$, which is the universal first-order asymptotic for the Stieltjes transform of *any* valid probability distribution.
