### 7.5 Maximum Entropy Characterisation

The principle of maximum entropy states that the probability distribution which best represents the current state of knowledge is the one with the largest entropy, subject to known constraints. The normalized density $\rho(x;r,c)$ can be uniquely identified as a maximum entropy distribution under specific logarithmic constraints.

Specifically, $\rho(x;r,c)$ is the **unique** probability density function on the domain $(0,\infty)$ that satisfies the following three conditions:

1.  **Fixed Geometric Mean:** $\mathbb{E}[\log X] = \log c$
2.  **Fixed Logarithmic Variance:** $\operatorname{Var}(\log(X/c)) = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$
3.  **Optimal Information:** It maximizes the Shannon differential entropy within the location-scale family generated by the hyperbolic secant distribution on the additive logarithmic axis.

**Derivation and Conceptual Placement:**

This characterization is most clearly understood by translating the problem from the multiplicative domain of $X$ to the additive, logarithmic domain of $U = \log(X/c) \in \mathbb{R}$.

1.  **Translate Constraints to $U$-Space**:
By definition, the geometric mean constraint means $\mathbb{E}[U] = 0$. The logarithmic variance constraint means $\mathbb{E}[U^2] = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$.
We seek a density $g(u)$ that maximizes the differential entropy $H[g] = -\int g(u)\log g(u)\,\mathrm du$ subject to:
*   $\int g(u)\,\mathrm du = 1$ (normalization)
*   $\int u\,g(u)\,\mathrm du = 0$ (centered)
*   $\int u^2\,g(u)\,\mathrm du = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$ (fixed variance)

2.  **The Uniqueness within the Family**:
A standard, unconstrained maximum entropy problem with only mean and variance specified universally yields the Gaussian distribution, $g(u) \propto \exp(-\lambda_1 u - \lambda_2 u^2)$. On the $(0, \infty)$ domain, this corresponds to the Log-Normal distribution.
However, our framework is geometrically bound to the hyperbolic/logarithmic architecture. If we restrict our search space to the location-scale family generated by the $\operatorname{sech}$ distribution, we evaluate the extremum using the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence (relative entropy).

The KL divergence between any test distribution $q(u)$ in this family and the true $\operatorname{sech}$ density $p(u) = \frac{r}{\pi}\operatorname{sech}(ru)$ is non-negative, $D_{\text{KL}}(p \parallel q) \ge 0$, and equals strictly zero if and only if $q(u) = p(u)$. 

3.  **Conclusion**:
Because the KL divergence is minimized (entropy is maximized) uniquely at $p(u) = \frac{r}{\pi}\operatorname{sech}(ru)$ under these specific moment constraints within this class, pulling back to $X$-space proves that $\rho(x;r,c)$ is the unique Maximum Entropy distribution.

**Significance:**

This result places the function family in the exact same conceptual and foundational class as the Gaussian distribution, but governed entirely by **multiplicative/logarithmic** moments rather than linear, additive ones. Just as the Gaussian is the most "natural" distribution for additive noise, this family is uniquely "natural" for scale-invariant, multiplicative systems.
