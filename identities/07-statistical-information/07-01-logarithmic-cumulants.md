## 7. Statistical & Information-Theoretic Properties

This section translates the analytical properties of the function into the language of probability and information theory. By examining the cumulants, entropy, and information geometry of the distribution, we reveal its connections to fundamental constants in number theory.

### 7.1 Log-Cumulants — Tangent Numbers and the Riemann Zeta Function

The cumulants of a probability distribution provide a sequence of descriptive constants (mean, variance, skewness, kurtosis, etc.) that are often more mathematically tractable than raw moments. For this family, the cumulants of the logarithmic variable $U = \log(X/c)$ are directly proportional to the **Tangent numbers**, and thus exactingly tied to the **Riemann Zeta function**.

Because the distribution of $U$ is symmetric around 0 (as established in §5.7), all odd-order log-cumulants ($\kappa_1, \kappa_3, \kappa_5, \dots$) are strictly zero. The non-zero, even-order log-cumulants are given by:

$$
\boxed{\; \kappa_{2n}\!\bigl(\log(X/c)\bigr) = T_{n}\left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n} = \frac{(2^{2n}-1)(2n-1)!\,\zeta(2n)}{2^{2n-1}\,r^{2n}} \;}
$$

where $T_n$ are the Tangent numbers (sequence A000182 in the OEIS): $T_1=1, T_2=2, T_3=16, T_4=272, T_5=7936, \dots$

**Derivation:**

The derivation extracts the cumulants from the Cumulant Generating Function (CGF), which is the natural logarithm of the Moment Generating Function (MGF).

1.  **Define the MGF and CGF**:  
Let $U = \log(X/c)$. The MGF of $U$ was derived in §5.6:
$$
M_U(s) = \mathbb{E}\left[e^{sU}\right] = \mathbb{E}\left[(X/c)^s\right] = \sec\left(\frac{s\pi}{2r}\right)
$$
The Cumulant Generating Function, $K(s)$, is the natural logarithm of the MGF:
$$
K(s) = \log(M_U(s)) = \log\sec\left(\frac{s\pi}{2r}\right)
$$

2.  **Expand the CGF into a Maclaurin Series**:  
By definition, the cumulants $\kappa_m$ are the coefficients in the Maclaurin series expansion of $K(s)$:
$$
K(s) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \frac{\kappa_m}{m!} s^m
$$
The classical Maclaurin series for $\log\sec(t)$ is generated by the Tangent numbers $T_n$:
$$
\log\sec(t) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{T_n}{(2n)!} t^{2n}
$$
*(Note: Since only even powers of $t$ appear in the series, it is immediately clear that $\kappa_{2n-1} = 0$, confirming the symmetry).*

3.  **Extract the Cumulants ($T_n$ form)**:  
Substitute $t = s\pi/(2r)$ into the series expansion:
$$
K(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{T_n}{(2n)!} \left(\frac{s\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2n)!} \left[ T_n \left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n} \right] s^{2n}
$$
Matching coefficients with the definition of $K(s)$ gives the first part of the boxed identity:
$$
\kappa_{2n} = T_n \left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n}
$$

4.  **Convert to the Riemann Zeta Form**:  
The Tangent numbers are deeply connected to the Bernoulli numbers $B_{2n}$ via the relation:
$$
T_n = \frac{2^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)|B_{2n}|}{2n}
$$
Euler's famous formula relates the even Bernoulli numbers to the Riemann Zeta function evaluated at even integers:
$$
\zeta(2n) = \frac{(2\pi)^{2n}|B_{2n}|}{2(2n)!} \quad \implies \quad |B_{2n}| = \frac{2(2n)! \zeta(2n)}{(2\pi)^{2n}}
$$
Substitute the expression for $|B_{2n}|$ into the formula for $T_n$:
$$
T_n = \frac{2^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)}{2n} \left[ \frac{2(2n)! \zeta(2n)}{2^{2n}\pi^{2n}} \right] = \frac{2(2^{2n}-1)(2n-1)! \zeta(2n)}{\pi^{2n}}
$$
Finally, substitute this expression for $T_n$ back into the cumulant formula from Step 3:
$$
\kappa_{2n} = \left[ \frac{2(2^{2n}-1)(2n-1)! \zeta(2n)}{\pi^{2n}} \right] \frac{\pi^{2n}}{2^{2n} r^{2n}} = \frac{(2^{2n}-1)(2n-1)! \zeta(2n)}{2^{2n-1} r^{2n}}
$$
This completes the derivation.

**Specific Low-Order Cumulants:**

*   **Variance ($n=1$):** $\kappa_2 = T_1\left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^2 = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$.
*(Using the Zeta form: $\frac{3 \cdot 1! \cdot \zeta(2)}{2r^2} = \frac{3(\pi^2/6)}{2r^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$)*.
*   **Kurtosis ($n=2$):** $\kappa_4 = T_2\left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^4 = 2\left(\frac{\pi^4}{16r^4}\right) = \frac{\pi^4}{8r^4}$.
*(Using the Zeta form: $\frac{15 \cdot 3! \cdot \zeta(4)}{8r^4} = \frac{90(\pi^4/90)}{8r^4} = \frac{\pi^4}{8r^4}$)*.
