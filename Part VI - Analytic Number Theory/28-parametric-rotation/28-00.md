# 28.1 Parametric Rotation of the Mellin Transform

By defining a continuously rotated evaluation line in the complex $s$-plane centered at the geometric symmetry axis $s=1$, we can interpolate smoothly between the distinct topologies of the framework.

Let $\theta \in [0, \pi/2]$ be the rotation angle, and $t \in \mathbb{R}$ be the real line parameter. We define the parametrically rotated Mellin transform evaluation, $L_{r,\theta}(t)$, as:
$$
\boxed{\; L_{r,\theta}(t) := \mathcal{M}\{f\}(1 + t e^{i\theta}; r, c=1) = \frac{\pi}{2r}\sec\!\left(\frac{\pi t e^{i\theta}}{2r}\right) \;}
$$

### Exposition
This identity defines a continuous complex rotation that maps the meridian of the torus (Circle B) to its longitude (Circle A). 
*   When $\theta = 0$, the line lies on the real axis, yielding a function with discrete, real poles. 
*   When $\theta = \pi/2$, the line becomes the vertical critical axis, yielding a purely real, bounded, everywhere-smooth function with no poles. 
*   For intermediate angles $\theta \in (0, \pi/2)$, the transform smoothly traverses the complex plane, providing a rigorous geometric homotopy between the discrete spectral space and the continuous spatial domain.

### Derivation

The derivation evaluates the known closed-form Mellin transform along the parametrized ray.

**Step 1: Define the Rotated Evaluation Line**
We anchor the rotation at the geometric center of symmetry for the base framework, $s=1$. We define a ray parameterized by a real variable $t$ and an angle $\theta$:
$$
s_\theta(t) = 1 + t e^{i\theta}, \quad t \in \mathbb{R}
$$

**Step 2: Substitute into the Mellin Transform**
Recall the secant form of the Mellin transform from §6.1, evaluated at $c=1$:
$$
\mathcal{M}\{f\}(s) = \frac{\pi}{2r} \sec\left(\frac{(s-1)\pi}{2r}\right)
$$
Substitute the parameterized line $s = s_\theta(t)$:
$$
L_{r,\theta}(t) = \frac{\pi}{2r} \sec\left(\frac{((1 + t e^{i\theta}) - 1)\pi}{2r}\right) = \frac{\pi}{2r} \sec\left(\frac{\pi t e^{i\theta}}{2r}\right)
$$

**Step 3: Evaluate at the Horizontal Boundary ($\theta = 0$)**
Substitute $\theta = 0$, meaning $e^{i(0)} = 1$. The evaluation line is the real axis $s = 1+t$.
$$
L_{r,0}(t) = \frac{\pi}{2r} \sec\left(\frac{\pi t}{2r}\right)
$$
This is the meridian function (Circle B). It is purely real for real $t$ and possesses an infinite sequence of discrete poles at $t = (2k+1)r$.

**Step 4: Evaluate at the Vertical Boundary ($\theta = \pi/2$)**
Substitute $\theta = \pi/2$, meaning $e^{i\pi/2} = i$. The evaluation line is the critical vertical axis $s = 1+it$.
$$
L_{r,\pi/2}(t) = \frac{\pi}{2r} \sec\left(\frac{i \pi t}{2r}\right)
$$
Using the standard identity $\sec(iz) = \operatorname{sech}(z)$, this evaluates to:
$$
L_{r,\pi/2}(t) = \frac{\pi}{2r} \operatorname{sech}\left(\frac{\pi t}{2r}\right)
$$
This is the longitudinal function (Circle A). It is purely real, strictly bounded between $(0, \pi/2r]$, and contains absolutely no poles or zeros for any real $t$. The parametric rotation $L_{r,\theta}(t)$ constitutes the exact, continuous analytic bridge between these two disparate boundary behaviors.
