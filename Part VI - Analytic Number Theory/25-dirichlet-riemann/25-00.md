\boxed{\; \mathcal M\bigl\{F_\chi\bigr\}(s) = L(s,\chi)\,\mathcal M\{f\}(s) \;}

This master identity proves that the continuous analytic structure of the Mellin transform acts as a universal geometric "scaffold" for discrete arithmetic. The choice of the character $\chi(n)$ dictates the arithmetic content. 
*   If $\chi(n) = 1$ for all $n$ (the principal character), the sum yields the **Riemann Zeta function**, $\zeta(s)$.
*   If $\chi(n) = (-1)^{n-1}$ (the alternating parity character), the sum yields the **Dirichlet Eta function**, $\eta(s)$.

### Derivation

The derivation relies entirely on the linearity of the Mellin transform integral and its fundamental operational scaling rule.

**Step 1: Apply the Mellin Scaling Rule**

$$
Substituting $a = n$, the transform of the $n$-th term in our sum, weighted by the character $\chi(n)$, is:
$$
\mathcal M\{\chi(n)f(nx)\}(s) = \chi(n) n^{-s}\mathcal M\{f\}(s)
$$

**Step 2: Integrate the Infinite Sum**
Assuming $\mathrm{Re}(s)$ is sufficiently large to guarantee uniform convergence, we apply the Mellin transform operator to the infinite sum $F_\chi(x)$, exchanging the order of integration and summation:
$$
\mathcal M\{F_\chi\}(s) = \mathcal M\left\{ \sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\chi(n)f(nx;r,c) \right\}(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \mathcal M\{\chi(n)f(nx;r,c)\}(s)
$$

**Step 3: Factor the Geometric Vacuum**
Substitute the individual scaled transforms from Step 1 into the infinite sum:
$$
\mathcal M\{F_\chi\}(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \chi(n) n^{-s} \mathcal M\{f\}(s)

Because the base continuous transform $\mathcal M\{f\}(s)$ is entirely independent of the discrete summation index $n$, it can be factored completely out of the sum:
$$
\mathcal M\{F_\chi\}(s) = \left( \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\chi(n)}{n^s} \right) \mathcal M\{f\}(s)

**Step 4: Identify the L-Function**

$$
L(s,\chi) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{\chi(n)}{n^s}

Substituting this definition yields the final boxed identity:
$$
\mathcal M\{F_\chi\}(s) = L(s,\chi)\,\mathcal M\{f\}(s)

Subtracting $1/2$ from both sides yields the exact summation identity. Substituting this back into the integral produces the second boxed identity.
