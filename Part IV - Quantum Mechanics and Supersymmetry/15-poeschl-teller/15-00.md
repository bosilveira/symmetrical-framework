# 15.1 The Quantum Ground State and Unitary Pushforward

In the context of quantum mechanics, we reinterpret the normalized probability density $\rho(x;r,c)$ as the Born probability density $|\psi_0(x)|^2$ of a stationary ground state. To analyze the true physics of this state, it must be transformed into the canonical logarithmic coordinate $u = \log(x/c)$ via a unitary transformation that preserves the $L^2$ inner product.

The ground-state wave function in the multiplicative spatial coordinate $x$ is:
$$
\boxed{\; \psi_{0}(x) = \sqrt{\rho(x;r,c)} = \sqrt{\frac{2r}{\pi}f(x;r,c)} \;}
$$
The unitarily transformed ground-state wave function in the additive coordinate $u$ is:
$$
\boxed{\; \chi(u) = \sqrt{\frac{r}{\pi}\operatorname{sech}(ru)} \;}
$$

### Exposition
Defining the wave function as the principal square root of the positive probability density guarantees that it is node-less, which is the defining topological characteristic of a quantum ground state. The transformation to $\chi(u)$ is not a simple coordinate substitution; it is a half-density pushforward. This ensures that the total probability integrates to 1 in both the $x$-space (with Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{d}x$) and the $u$-space (with Lebesgue measure $\mathrm{d}u$).

### Derivation

**Step 1: Define the Spatial Wave Function**
We define the ground state $\psi_0(x)$ such that its squared modulus yields the normalized density from §1.7:
$$
|\psi_0(x)|^2 = \rho(x;r,c) = \frac{2r}{\pi}f(x;r,c)
$$
Taking the positive square root (appropriate for a node-less ground state) yields the first boxed formula. By the total mass integral (§5.3), this state is natively normalized:
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} |\psi_0(x)|^2 \,\mathrm{d}x = \int_{0}^{\infty} \rho(x;r,c) \,\mathrm{d}x = 1
$$

**Step 2: Define the Unitary Transformation**
Let $u = \log(x/c)$, which implies $x = c e^u$. The differential measure transforms as $\mathrm{d}x = c e^u \mathrm{d}u$. 
To preserve the $L^2$ norm under this coordinate change, the wave function must absorb the square root of the Jacobian of the transformation:
$$
\int_{0}^{\infty} |\psi_0(x)|^2 \,\mathrm{d}x = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} |\psi_0(ce^u)|^2 (ce^u \mathrm{d}u) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \left| \psi_0(ce^u)\sqrt{ce^u} \right|^2 \mathrm{d}u
$$
This dictates that the unitarily transformed wave function $\chi(u)$ must be defined as:
$$
\chi(u) := \psi_{0}(ce^{u})\sqrt{ce^{u}}
$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the Transformed State**
We substitute the explicit formula for $\psi_0(x)$ into this transformation, utilizing the Rosetta Stone identity $f(ce^u) = \frac{1}{2ce^u}\operatorname{sech}(ru)$ (from §1.3):
$$
\chi(u) = \sqrt{\frac{2r}{\pi} f(ce^u; r, c)} \cdot \sqrt{ce^u} = \sqrt{\frac{2r}{\pi} \left( \frac{1}{2ce^u}\operatorname{sech}(ru) \right)} \cdot \sqrt{ce^u}
$$

**Step 4: Algebraic Simplification**
Combine the terms under the square root. The Jacobian factor $ce^u$ perfectly cancels the $ce^u$ term in the denominator of the linearized function:
$$
\chi(u) = \sqrt{ \frac{2r}{\pi} \frac{1}{2ce^u} \operatorname{sech}(ru) \cdot ce^u } = \sqrt{\frac{r}{\pi} \operatorname{sech}(ru)}
$$
This yields the final boxed formula for $\chi(u)$.

# 15.2 The SUSY Superpotential

The fundamental operator in Supersymmetric Quantum Mechanics (SUSY QM) is the superpotential, defined as the negative logarithmic derivative of the ground-state wave function. It serves to factorize the Schrödinger Hamiltonian into first-order lowering and raising operators.

The superpotential in the original spatial coordinate $x$ is:
$$
\boxed{\; W_{\text{susy}}(x) := -\frac{\psi_{0}'(x)}{\psi_{0}(x)} = \frac{1}{2x} + \frac{r}{2x}\tanh\bigl(r\log(x/c)\bigr) \;}
$$
The corresponding effective superpotential in the canonical coordinate $u = \log(x/c)$ is:
$$
\boxed{\; \widetilde{W}_{\text{susy}}(u) := -\frac{\chi'(u)}{\chi(u)} = \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru) \;}
$$

### Exposition
The superpotential $W_{\text{susy}}$ governs the localized quantum dynamics of the system. While the $x$-space formulation is cluttered by a $1/(2x)$ term originating from the multiplicative measure's Jacobian, the $u$-space superpotential is remarkably pure. It is exactly proportional to the conformal phase $W = \tanh(ru)$, confirming that the phase function of the geometric framework acts strictly as the quantum-mechanical force driving the system toward its ground state.

### Derivation

**Step 1: Derive the Spatial Superpotential $W_{\text{susy}}(x)$**
By definition, $\psi_0(x) \propto f(x)^{1/2}$. Using the chain rule, the logarithmic derivative of the wave function is exactly half the logarithmic derivative of the base function $f(x)$:
$$
-\frac{\psi_0'(x)}{\psi_0(x)} = -\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x} \log\left(\sqrt{f(x)}\right) = -\frac{1}{2} \frac{f'(x)}{f(x)}
$$
From the Euler-Cauchy identity (§3.2), we have already established the logarithmic derivative:
$$
\frac{f'(x)}{f(x)} = -\frac{1 + rW(x)}{x} = -\frac{1 + r\tanh(r\log(x/c))}{x}
$$
Substitute this into the superpotential definition:
$$
W_{\text{susy}}(x) = -\frac{1}{2} \left[ -\frac{1 + r\tanh(r\log(x/c))}{x} \right] = \frac{1}{2x} + \frac{r}{2x}\tanh\bigl(r\log(x/c)\bigr)
$$

**Step 2: Derive the Canonical Superpotential $\widetilde{W}_{\text{susy}}(u)$**
We apply the same definition to the unitarily transformed wave function $\chi(u)$ derived in §15.1:
$$
\widetilde{W}_{\text{susy}}(u) = -\frac{\chi'(u)}{\chi(u)} = -\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} \log\bigl(\chi(u)\bigr)
$$
Substitute $\chi(u) = \sqrt{\frac{r}{\pi}\operatorname{sech}(ru)}$:
$$
\log\bigl(\chi(u)\bigr) = \frac{1}{2} \log\left(\frac{r}{\pi}\operatorname{sech}(ru)\right) = \frac{1}{2} \log\left(\frac{r}{\pi}\right) + \frac{1}{2}\log(\operatorname{sech}(ru))
$$
Differentiate with respect to $u$. The constant term vanishes, and we use the standard derivative $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\log(\operatorname{sech}(ru)) = -r\tanh(ru)$:
$$
\widetilde{W}_{\text{susy}}(u) = -\left[ \frac{1}{2} (-r\tanh(ru)) \right] = \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)
$$
This completes the derivation.

# 15.3 The Pöschl-Teller Connection

The unitary transformation into the canonical logarithmic coordinate system reveals that the algebraic complexity of the symmetrical function collapses exactly into one of the most famous, exactly solvable models in theoretical physics.

The transformed ground state $\chi(u) \propto \operatorname{sech}^{1/2}(ru)$ is exactly the ground-state wave function for the **modified Pöschl-Teller potential**.

### Exposition
The Pöschl-Teller potential well is a cornerstone of quantum mechanics and soliton theory. For specific integer tunings, it is a completely "reflectionless" potential, meaning wave packets scatter through it without generating any backward-propagating waves. 

The fact that the statistical and geometric framework natively pushes forward into this specific solitonic ground state proves that the underlying geometry possesses the highest possible degree of physical integrability.

### The Physical Constraint ($r > 2$)
While the probability density $\rho(x;r,c)$ and the wave function $\psi_0(x)$ are normalizable for any $r > 0$, treating this system as a standard quantum-mechanical bound state imposes a stricter limit. 

In quantum mechanics, the expected kinetic energy is proportional to $\int |\psi_0'(x)|^2 \mathrm{d}x$. 
By examining the derivative $f'(x)$ near the origin ($x \to 0^+$), one finds that $f'(x) \sim x^{r-2}$. For the squared gradient $|f'(x)/f(x)^{1/2}|^2 \sim x^{r-3}$ to be integrable at the origin (ensuring the state has finite kinetic energy), we require $r - 3 > -1$, which implies strictly:
$$
r > 2
$$
For $r \le 2$, the quantum state is no longer physically bound with finite energy, marking $r=2$ (the Moonshine locus) as an absolute quantum critical point of the framework.
