# 17.1 Darboux Transformation and Partner Potentials

The Supersymmetric partner potentials, $V_-(u)$ and $V_+(u)$, derived via the ladder operators in §16.3, are connected by a rigorous mathematical operation known as the **Darboux transformation**. This transformation generates a new, isospectral Hamiltonian by acting directly on the logarithm of the ground-state wave function.

For a Hamiltonian $H_- = -\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} + V_-(u)$ with exact ground state $\chi(u)$, the Darboux-transformed partner potential $V_+(u)$ is uniquely determined by:
$$
\boxed{\; V_+(u) = V_-(u) - 2 \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} \log \chi(u) \;}
$$

### Exposition

The Darboux transformation is the analytical engine of SUSY Quantum Mechanics. It proves that one does not need the intermediate step of factoring the Hamiltonian into $A$ and $A^\dagger$ to find the partner potential; instead, the partner potential is generated directly by the curvature (the second logarithmic derivative) of the ground state itself. This reveals that the physical states of the system intrinsically encode the geometry of their partner manifolds.

### Derivation

We derive this identity by explicitly evaluating the Darboux transform of the $u$-space ground state established in §15.1, and showing it perfectly recovers the partner potential $V_+(u)$ from §16.3.

**Step 1: Identify the Ground State and Initial Potential**
From §15.1, the canonical ground-state wave function is:
$$
\chi(u) = \sqrt{\frac{r}{\pi}} \operatorname{sech}^{1/2}(ru)
$$
From §16.3, the primary potential is:
$$
V_-(u) = \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{3r^2}{4}\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
$$

**Step 2: Compute the Logarithm of the Ground State**
Take the natural logarithm of $\chi(u)$:
$$
\log \chi(u) = \log\left( \sqrt{\frac{r}{\pi}} \operatorname{sech}^{1/2}(ru) \right) = \frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{r}{\pi}\right) + \frac{1}{2}\log(\operatorname{sech}(ru))
$$

**Step 3: Compute the Second Derivative**
We differentiate this expression twice with respect to $u$.
*   **First derivative:** The constant term vanishes. Using $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\log(\operatorname{sech}(ru)) = -r\tanh(ru)$:
    $$
    \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} \log \chi(u) = \frac{1}{2} (-r\tanh(ru)) = -\frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)
    $$
*   **Second derivative:** Using $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\tanh(ru) = r\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)$:
    $$
    \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} \log \chi(u) = -\frac{r}{2} \bigl( r\operatorname{sech}^2(ru) \bigr) = -\frac{r^2}{2}\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
    $$

**Step 4: Apply the Darboux Transformation**
Substitute the second derivative into the Darboux formula:
$$
V_+(u) = V_-(u) - 2 \left( -\frac{r^2}{2}\operatorname{sech}^2(ru) \right) = V_-(u) + r^2\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
$$

**Step 5: Assemble and Verify**
Substitute the explicit form of $V_-(u)$ from Step 1:
$$
V_+(u) = \left( \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{3r^2}{4}\operatorname{sech}^2(ru) \right) + r^2\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
$$
$$
= \frac{r^2}{4} + \left( 1 - \frac{3}{4} \right) r^2\operatorname{sech}^2(ru) = \frac{r^2}{4} + \frac{r^2}{4}\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
$$
This result is exactly the $V_+(u)$ barrier potential derived via the operator product $A A^\dagger$ in §16.3. The equivalence is rigorously established.

# 17.2 The Darboux Generator (Logarithmic Second Derivative of $f$)

The previous section demonstrated that the Darboux transformation depends on the second logarithmic derivative of the *square root* of the function. However, the exact Darboux potential shift term can be generated directly and beautifully from the unaltered, base symmetrical function itself.

In the canonical coordinate $u = \log(x/c)$, the second logarithmic derivative of the linearized base function evaluates exactly to the negative square of the hyperbolic core:
$$
\boxed{\; \frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} \log f(ce^u; r, c) = -r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru) = -r^2 S(u)^2 \;}
$$

### Exposition

This is a remarkably clean structural identity. It proves that the unmodified algebraic function $f(x; r, c)$ acts as the pure geometric generator (the prepotential) for the quantum mechanical system. One does not need to transition to probability amplitudes (square roots) to find the Schrödinger potential structure; the exact solitonic well $-r^2\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)$ is inherently encoded in the curvature of $\log f$.

### Derivation

The derivation leverages the explicit linearized form of the function defined in §1.3.

**Step 1: Recall the Linearized Function**
From §1.3, substituting $x = ce^u$ into the base function yields the linearized kernel:
$$
g(u) := f(ce^u; r, c) = \frac{\operatorname{sech}(ru)}{2ce^u}
$$

**Step 2: Compute the Logarithm**
Take the natural logarithm of $g(u)$:
$$
\log f(ce^u; r, c) = \log\left( \frac{\operatorname{sech}(ru)}{2ce^u} \right) = \log(\operatorname{sech}(ru)) - \log(2c) - u
$$

**Step 3: Compute the First Derivative**
Differentiate with respect to $u$:
*   $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} \log(\operatorname{sech}(ru)) = -r\tanh(ru)$
*   $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} (-\log(2c)) = 0$
*   $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} (-u) = -1$

Thus, the first derivative is:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} \log f(ce^u; r, c) = -r\tanh(ru) - 1
$$
*(Note: This perfectly matches the Euler-Cauchy identity rearranged as $\frac{x f'}{f} = -rW - 1$ from §3.2).*

**Step 4: Compute the Second Derivative**
Differentiate the result from Step 3 with respect to $u$. The constant $-1$ vanishes:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} \log f(ce^u; r, c) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} \bigl( -r\tanh(ru) \bigr) = -r\bigl( r\operatorname{sech}^2(ru) \bigr)
$$
$$
= -r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
$$
Substituting the Rosetta Stone variable $S(u) = \operatorname{sech}(ru)$ yields the final boxed identity.

# 17.3 Shape Invariance and the SUSY Tower

The Pöschl-Teller potentials generated by this framework possess a remarkable mathematical property known as **Shape Invariance**. When a Darboux transformation is applied to these potentials, the resulting partner potential has the exact same mathematical "shape," differing only by a discrete shift in a parameter. This allows the Darboux transformation to be iterated, generating an infinite "tower" of exactly solvable Hamiltonians.

Let the generalized potential at level $k$ be defined as:
$$
\boxed{\; V^{(k)}(u) = C - k(k+1)r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru) \;}
$$
If $\psi_k(u)$ is the ground state of $V^{(k)}(u)$, applying the Darboux transformation rigorously steps the parameter down to $k-1$:
$$
\boxed{\; V^{(k)}(u) - 2\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} \log \psi_k(u) = V^{(k-1)}(u) \;}
$$

### Exposition

Shape invariance is the fundamental mechanism that guarantees a quantum system is exactly solvable for all its energy levels, not just the ground state. Because the partner potentials retain the same algebraic form, one can chain the SUSY ladder operators together to algebraically construct every excited state wave function and eigenvalue without ever solving a differential equation directly. The parameter $k$ acts as the vertical index of this hierarchy.

### Derivation

We demonstrate the shape-invariant parameter shift explicitly.

**Step 1: The Ground State at Level $k$**
For the generalized Pöschl-Teller potential $V^{(k)}(u) \propto -k(k+1)r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru)$, the exact ground-state wave function is known to be proportional to the $k$-th power of the hyperbolic secant:
$$
\psi_k(u) \propto \operatorname{sech}^k(ru)
$$

**Step 2: Compute the Darboux Generator**
We compute the second logarithmic derivative of this ground state.
$$
\log \psi_k(u) = \log\bigl(\operatorname{sech}^k(ru)\bigr) + \text{const} = k \log(\operatorname{sech}(ru)) + \text{const}
$$
First derivative:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} \log \psi_k(u) = k (-r\tanh(ru)) = -kr\tanh(ru)
$$
Second derivative:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} \log \psi_k(u) = -kr (r\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)) = -kr^2\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
$$

**Step 3: Apply the Darboux Transformation**
Substitute the original potential $V^{(k)}$ and the evaluated second derivative into the Darboux formula:
$$
\widetilde{V}(u) = V^{(k)}(u) - 2\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} \log \psi_k(u)
$$
$$
= \left[ C - k(k+1)r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru) \right] - 2\left( -kr^2\operatorname{sech}^2(ru) \right)
$$
$$
= C - \left[ k(k+1) - 2k \right] r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
$$

**Step 4: Simplify the Polynomial in $k$**
Expand the bracketed polynomial:
$$
k(k+1) - 2k = k^2 + k - 2k = k^2 - k = k(k-1)
$$
This is equivalent to $(k-1)\bigl((k-1)+1\bigr)$.

**Step 5: Conclude Shape Invariance**
Substitute this simplified polynomial back into the potential:
$$
\widetilde{V}(u) = C - (k-1)k \,r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru) = V^{(k-1)}(u)
$$
This completes the proof. Applying the Darboux transformation with the system's ground state perfectly shifts the amplitude parameter from $k$ to $k-1$. 

*(Note: The primary potential $V_-(u)$ from §16.3 corresponds exactly to $k=1/2$, where $k(k+1) = \frac{1}{2}(\frac{3}{2}) = \frac{3}{4}$, matching the $\frac{3r^2}{4}$ coefficient).*

# 17.4 Jost Functions and the Power Tower

Because the potentials are shape-invariant, the exact eigenstates (Jost functions) for the entire hierarchy of solvable potentials can be constructed directly from the base symmetrical function. 

The ground state wave function for the $k$-th level potential, $V^{(k)}(u)$, is exactly proportional to the $k$-th power of the Rosetta Stone object:

$$
\boxed{\; \psi_k(u) \propto S(u)^k = \left[ 2x f(x; r, c) \right]^k \;}
$$

### Exposition

This elegantly closes the loop between the quantum mechanical hierarchy and the algebraic framework. It proves that the operation of taking integer powers of the base function $f(x)$—which we investigated analytically via the $L^n$ norm Beta integrals (§5.5) and the generalized Mellin transforms (§6.5)—has a direct, rigorous physical meaning: $f(x)^k$ generates the quantum eigenstate for the $k$-th step of the SUSY Darboux tower. 

### Derivation

The derivation relies on verifying that $\psi_k(u) = S^k$ satisfies the time-independent Schrödinger equation for the specified potential $V^{(k)}$.

**Step 1: Set up the Schrödinger Equation**
We must show that $H^{(k)} \psi_k = E_k \psi_k$, which means:
$$
-\psi_k''(u) + V^{(k)}(u) \psi_k(u) = E_k \psi_k(u)
$$
where $V^{(k)}(u) = -k(k+1)r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru)$ (ignoring the constant energy offset $C$ for clarity, which simply shifts the eigenvalue).

**Step 2: Differentiate the Proposed State**
Let $\psi_k(u) = \operatorname{sech}^k(ru) = S^k$. 
*   **First derivative:**
    $$
    \psi_k'(u) = k \operatorname{sech}^{k-1}(ru) \cdot (-r\operatorname{sech}(ru)\tanh(ru)) = -kr S^k \tanh(ru)
    $$
*   **Second derivative:** Apply the product rule.
    $$
    \psi_k''(u) = -kr \left[ (S^k)' \tanh(ru) + S^k (\tanh(ru))' \right]
    $$
    Using $(S^k)' = -krS^k\tanh(ru)$ and $(\tanh(ru))' = r\operatorname{sech}^2(ru) = rS^2$:
    $$
    \psi_k''(u) = -kr \left[ (-kr S^k \tanh^2(ru)) + S^k (rS^2) \right]
    $$
    $$
    = k^2r^2 S^k \tanh^2(ru) - kr^2 S^{k+2}
    $$
    Substitute $\tanh^2(ru) = 1 - S^2$:
    $$
    \psi_k''(u) = k^2r^2 S^k (1 - S^2) - kr^2 S^{k+2} = k^2r^2 S^k - k^2r^2 S^{k+2} - kr^2 S^{k+2}
    $$
    $$
    = r^2 S^k \left( k^2 - k^2 S^2 - k S^2 \right) = r^2 S^k \left( k^2 - k(k+1)S^2 \right)
    $$

**Step 3: Substitute into the Schrödinger Equation**
Evaluate the left-hand side of the Schrödinger equation, $-\psi_k'' + V^{(k)}\psi_k$:
$$
- \left[ r^2 S^k \left( k^2 - k(k+1)S^2 \right) \right] + \left[ -k(k+1)r^2 S^2 \right] S^k
$$
$$
= -k^2 r^2 S^k + k(k+1)r^2 S^{k+2} - k(k+1)r^2 S^{k+2}
$$
The $S^{k+2}$ terms cancel perfectly, leaving:
$$
= -k^2 r^2 S^k = (-k^2 r^2) \psi_k(u)
$$

**Step 4: Conclude**
This shows that $\psi_k(u) = S^k$ is an exact eigenstate with the energy eigenvalue $E_k = -k^2 r^2$. Substituting the Rosetta Stone identity $S = 2xf(x;r,c)$ completes the proof of the boxed identity.

# 17.5 Affine Weyl Group and the Dressing Chain

The iteration of the Darboux transformation generates a hierarchy of potentials known as a **Dressing Chain**. In the symmetrical framework, the operators that shift the parameters of this chain form a representation of an infinite discrete symmetry group, specifically the extended affine Weyl group $\widetilde{W}(A_1^{(1)})$.

$$
\boxed{\; T(k) = k + 1 \qquad \text{and} \qquad S(k) = -k \;}

These operators generate the affine Weyl group, dictating the symmetries of the entire quantum hierarchy.

### Exposition
This result elevates the quantum mechanical ladder operators into the realm of Lie theory and algebraic geometry. It proves that the "shape invariance" of the Pöschl-Teller potentials is actually the signature of a deeper group-theoretic symmetry. The sequence of potentials forms an "open" dressing chain, meaning the chain does not loop back on itself periodically. This exact Weyl group structure is the same group that governs the Bäcklund transformations of the Painlevé equations, providing the rigorous theoretical bridge between the quantum mechanics of Part IV and the integrable systems of Part V.

### Derivation

**Step 1: The Translation Generator (Darboux Shift)**
As proven in §17.3, applying the Darboux transformation to the potential $V^{(k)}$ using its ground state $\psi_k$ generates the potential $V^{(k-1)}$. Conversely, using the adjoint operators steps the potential up.
We define the translation operator $T$ such that it shifts the system to the next level of the SUSY tower:
$$
T : V^{(k)} \longmapsto V^{(k+1)} \quad \implies \quad T(k) = k+1
$$

**Step 2: The Reflection Generator (Parity Symmetry)**
We examine the explicit form of the generalized potential from §17.3 (ignoring the constant offset):
$$
V^{(k)}(u) = -k(k+1)r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru)
$$
This potential depends on the parameter $k$ strictly through the combination $k(k+1)$.
Notice that if we replace $k$ with $-(k+1)$, the coefficient becomes:
$$
(-k-1)(-k-1+1) = -(k+1)(-k) = k(k+1)

Therefore, the potential is perfectly invariant under the transformation $k \mapsto -k-1$.

Because $n r^2 \operatorname{sech}^2(ru)$ is strictly positive for all $n \ge 1$ and real $u$, the sum can never be zero. Therefore, the dressing chain of the symmetrical framework is strictly **open**. It ascends infinitely without looping, corresponding to an infinite lattice of distinct soliton states.
