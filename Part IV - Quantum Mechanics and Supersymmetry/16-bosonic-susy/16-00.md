# 16.1 Bosonic Ladder Operators and Ground State Annihilation

In Supersymmetric Quantum Mechanics (SUSY QM), the factorisation of the Hamiltonian is achieved by defining first-order differential operators, often called ladder or charge operators. For our framework, these operators are constructed natively in the canonical logarithmic coordinate $u = \log(x/c)$ using the exact superpotential of the system.

Let $\widetilde{W}(u) = \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)$ be the canonical superpotential. The bosonic annihilation operator $A$ and creation operator $A^\dagger$ are defined as:

$$
\boxed{\; A = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \widetilde{W}(u) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru) \;}
$$
$$
\boxed{\; A^\dagger = -\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \widetilde{W}(u) = -\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru) \;}
$$

### Exposition
These first-order operators act on the Hilbert space of wave functions in $u$-space. By construction, the annihilation operator $A$ must completely annihilate the exact ground state wave function of the system. This property guarantees that the ground state energy of the associated primary Hamiltonian is exactly zero, a hallmark of unbroken supersymmetry.

### Derivation

We demonstrate that the operator $A$ annihilates the unitarily transformed ground state $\chi(u)$ derived in §15.1.

**Step 1: Recall the Ground State Wave Function**
From §15.1, the ground state in the canonical coordinate is:
$$
\chi(u) = \sqrt{\frac{r}{\pi}} \mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru)
$$
We can ignore the normalization constant $\sqrt{r/\pi}$ for the purpose of the annihilation proof, focusing on the core functional dependence $\mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru)$.

**Step 2: Apply the Annihilation Operator**
Apply the operator $A = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \widetilde{W}(u)$ to the state $\chi(u)$:
$$
A \chi(u) = \left( \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru) \right) \left[ \mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru) \right]
$$
$$
= \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\left[ \mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru) \right] + \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)\mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru)
$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the Derivative**
Use the chain rule to differentiate the fractional power of the hyperbolic secant:
$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\left[ \mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru) \right] = \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{sech}^{-1/2}(ru) \cdot \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\bigl[\mathrm{sech}(ru)\bigr]
$$
Substituting the standard derivative $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\mathrm{sech}(ru) = -r\mathrm{sech}(ru)\tanh(ru)$:
$$
= \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{sech}^{-1/2}(ru) \bigl[ -r\mathrm{sech}(ru)\tanh(ru) \bigr]
$$
$$
= -\frac{r}{2}\mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru)\tanh(ru)
$$

**Step 4: Combine and Conclude**
Substitute the evaluated derivative back into the expression from Step 2:
$$
A \chi(u) = -\frac{r}{2}\mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru)\tanh(ru) + \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)\mathrm{sech}^{1/2}(ru) = 0
$$
The terms cancel perfectly. This proves that $\chi(u)$ is the zero-mode of the operator $A$, establishing it rigorously as the SUSY ground state.

# 16.2 The Position-Dependent Commutator ($w$-Deformed Algebra)

The fundamental difference between the symmetrical framework's quantum mechanics and a standard harmonic oscillator lies in the algebraic commutator of its ladder operators. Rather than evaluating to a constant, the commutator yields a localized, position-dependent function.

The commutator of the bosonic ladder operators $[A, A^\dagger] = A A^\dagger - A^\dagger A$ evaluates exactly to:

$$
\boxed{\; [A, A^\dagger] = r^2 \mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \;}
$$

### Exposition
For a standard quantum harmonic oscillator, the superpotential is linear ($W(u) \propto u$), and the commutator $[A, A^\dagger]$ yields a constant (the identity operator), generating the standard Heisenberg algebra. 

In our framework, the superpotential is non-linear ($W(u) \propto \tanh(ru)$). As a result, the commutator produces a sharply localized function proportional to the square of the Rosetta Stone core function $S^2 = \mathrm{sech}^2(ru)$. This specific algebraic structure is known as a **$w$-deformed algebra** (or polynomial Heisenberg algebra), which is the underlying algebraic signature of Pöschl-Teller-type solitonic systems.

### Derivation

The derivation requires expanding the commutator bracket using the differential definitions of $A$ and $A^\dagger$.

**Step 1: Define the Commutator Expansion**
Let $\phi(u)$ be an arbitrary test wave function. We apply the commutator $[A, A^\dagger] = A A^\dagger - A^\dagger A$ to $\phi(u)$:
$$
[A, A^\dagger]\phi = A(A^\dagger \phi) - A^\dagger(A \phi)
$$

**Step 2: Evaluate $A A^\dagger \phi$**
Using $A = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \widetilde{W}$ and $A^\dagger = -\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \widetilde{W}$:
$$
A(A^\dagger \phi) = \left( \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \widetilde{W} \right) \left( -\phi' + \widetilde{W}\phi \right)
$$
Applying the derivative via the product rule:
$$
= \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}(-\phi' + \widetilde{W}\phi) + \widetilde{W}(-\phi' + \widetilde{W}\phi)
$$
$$
= -\phi'' + \widetilde{W}'\phi + \widetilde{W}\phi' - \widetilde{W}\phi' + \widetilde{W}^2\phi = -\phi'' + \widetilde{W}'\phi + \widetilde{W}^2\phi
$$

**Step 3: Evaluate $A^\dagger A \phi$**
$$
A^\dagger(A \phi) = \left( -\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} + \widetilde{W} \right) \left( \phi' + \widetilde{W}\phi \right)
$$
Applying the negative derivative:
$$
= -\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}(\phi' + \widetilde{W}\phi) + \widetilde{W}(\phi' + \widetilde{W}\phi)
$$
$$
= -\phi'' - \widetilde{W}'\phi - \widetilde{W}\phi' + \widetilde{W}\phi' + \widetilde{W}^2\phi = -\phi'' - \widetilde{W}'\phi + \widetilde{W}^2\phi
$$

**Step 4: Subtract the Terms**
Subtract the result of Step 3 from Step 2:
$$
[A, A^\dagger]\phi = \left( -\phi'' + \widetilde{W}'\phi + \widetilde{W}^2\phi \right) - \left( -\phi'' - \widetilde{W}'\phi + \widetilde{W}^2\phi \right)
$$
The second derivative ($-\phi''$) and the squared potential ($\widetilde{W}^2\phi$) terms cancel exactly, leaving:
$$
[A, A^\dagger]\phi = 2\widetilde{W}'\phi
$$
Removing the test function $\phi$ gives the operator identity $[A, A^\dagger] = 2\widetilde{W}'(u)$.

**Step 5: Substitute the Framework's Superpotential**
Recall that $\widetilde{W}(u) = \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)$. We compute its derivative:
$$
\widetilde{W}'(u) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\left( \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru) \right) = \frac{r}{2} \cdot r\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) = \frac{r^2}{2}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
$$
Substitute this into the operator identity from Step 4:
$$
[A, A^\dagger] = 2 \left( \frac{r^2}{2}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \right) = r^2\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
$$
This yields the final boxed equation.

# 16.3 Partner Hamiltonians and Intertwining

The product of the ladder operators in opposite orders generates a pair of SUSY partner Hamiltonians, $H_-$ and $H_+$. These Hamiltonians are isospectral (sharing identical energy levels above the ground state) and are linked by a fundamental algebraic relation known as intertwining.

The primary Hamiltonian (which possesses the zero-energy ground state) is:
$$
\boxed{\; H_- := A^\dagger A = -\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} + \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{3r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \;}
$$
The partner Hamiltonian (which possesses no bound states) is:
$$
\boxed{\; H_+ := A A^\dagger = -\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} + \frac{r^2}{4} + \frac{r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \;}
$$

These operators satisfy the **Intertwining Relation**:
$$
\boxed{\; H_- A^\dagger = A^\dagger H_+ \qquad \text{and} \qquad H_+ A = A H_- \;}
$$

### Exposition
This step definitively derives the reflectionless Pöschl-Teller potential. $H_-$ describes a deep attractive quantum well containing exactly one bound state (our $E_0=0$ ground state). $H_+$, on the other hand, describes a repulsive scattering barrier. The intertwining relations rigorously prove that if $\psi$ is an eigenstate of $H_+$ with energy $E$, then $A^\dagger \psi$ is an eigenstate of $H_-$ with the exact same energy $E$. This establishes an exact spectral equivalence between the well and the barrier for all continuous scattering states.

### Derivation

**Step 1: Construct the Generic Hamiltonians**
From the expansions in §16.2, we established the operator products applied to a test function $\phi$:
*   $A^\dagger A = -\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} + (\widetilde{W}^2 - \widetilde{W}')$
*   $A A^\dagger = -\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} + (\widetilde{W}^2 + \widetilde{W}')$

Therefore, the effective potentials are $V_-(u) = \widetilde{W}^2 - \widetilde{W}'$ and $V_+(u) = \widetilde{W}^2 + \widetilde{W}'$.

**Step 2: Evaluate the Potential Components**
Using the superpotential $\widetilde{W}(u) = \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)$:
*   **The Squared Term:**
    $$
    \widetilde{W}^2(u) = \frac{r^2}{4}\tanh^2(ru)
    $$
    Using the identity $\tanh^2(ru) = 1 - \mathrm{sech}^2(ru)$, we rewrite this as:
    $$
    \widetilde{W}^2(u) = \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
    $$
*   **The Derivative Term:** As computed in §16.2:
    $$
    \widetilde{W}'(u) = \frac{r^2}{2}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
    $$

**Step 3: Assemble the Primary Potential $V_-(u)$**
Subtract the derivative term from the squared term:
$$
V_-(u) = \widetilde{W}^2 - \widetilde{W}' = \left( \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \right) - \left( \frac{r^2}{2}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \right)
$$
$$
= \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{3r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
$$
This gives the explicit operator $H_-$.

**Step 4: Assemble the Partner Potential $V_+(u)$**
Add the derivative term to the squared term:
$$
V_+(u) = \widetilde{W}^2 + \widetilde{W}' = \left( \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \right) + \left( \frac{r^2}{2}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \right)
$$
$$
= \frac{r^2}{4} + \frac{r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
$$
This gives the explicit operator $H_+$.

**Step 5: Prove the Intertwining Relation**
The intertwining relation is an immediate algebraic consequence of operator associativity.
Substitute the definitions $H_- = A^\dagger A$ and $H_+ = A A^\dagger$ into the first intertwining equation:
$$
H_- A^\dagger = (A^\dagger A) A^\dagger = A^\dagger (A A^\dagger) = A^\dagger H_+
$$
Similarly, for the second equation:
$$
H_+ A = (A A^\dagger) A = A (A^\dagger A) = A H_-
$$
This completes the derivation.

# 16.4 The Two Riccati Levels

The physical SUSY superpotential $W_2(u) = \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)$ belongs to the "Level 2" of a differential hierarchy. It is derived from the wave function $\psi_0 = g^{1/2}$. There exists a simpler "Level 1" Riccati equation derived directly from the linearized function $g(u) = \mathrm{sech}(ru)$, which generates a trivial free-particle potential.

**Level 1 (From $g$):**
$$
W_1(u) := \frac{g'(u)}{g(u)} = -r\tanh(ru) \quad \implies \quad \boxed{ W_1' = W_1^2 - r^2 }
$$
**Level 2 (From $\psi_0 = g^{1/2}$):**
$$
W_2(u) := \frac{\psi_0'(u)}{\psi_0(u)} = \frac{1}{2}W_1(u) \quad \implies \quad \boxed{ W_2' \neq W_2^2 + \text{const} }
$$

### Exposition
This hierarchy clarifies the origin of the fractional coefficients in the Pöschl-Teller potential. The Level 1 Riccati equation is autonomous (a constant difference between $W_1'$ and $W_1^2$), leading to a completely flat (constant) potential for its primary Hamiltonian. Taking the square root of $g$ to form a valid probability amplitude pushes the system to Level 2, breaking the autonomous nature of the Riccati equation and generating the reflectionless potential well.

### Derivation

**Step 1: The Level 1 Riccati Equation**
From the derivative hierarchy (§3.1), we have $g'(u) = -rg(u)\tanh(ru)$. Dividing by $g(u)$ gives the Level 1 logarithmic derivative:
$$
W_1(u) = -r\tanh(ru)
$$
We compute the derivative $W_1'$:
$$
W_1'(u) = -r^2\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
$$
Using the identity $\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) = 1 - \tanh^2(ru)$, we have:
$$
W_1'(u) = -r^2(1 - \tanh^2(ru)) = r^2\tanh^2(ru) - r^2
$$
Recognizing that $r^2\tanh^2(ru) = W_1(u)^2$, we obtain the autonomous Level 1 Riccati equation:
$$
W_1' = W_1^2 - r^2
$$

**Step 2: Level 1 Partner Potentials**
If we treat $W_1(u)$ as a superpotential and construct its SUSY partner potentials $V_{1}^{\pm} = W_1^2 \pm W_1'$:
*   $V_{1}^-(u) = W_1^2 - W_1' = W_1^2 - (W_1^2 - r^2) = r^2$
*   $V_{1}^+(u) = W_1^2 + W_1' = r^2\tanh^2(ru) - r^2\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) = r^2 - 2r^2\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)$

The primary potential $V_1^-$ is a pure constant ($r^2$), representing a free particle with a shifted energy spectrum.

**Step 3: Transition to Level 2**
The physical quantum ground state is $\psi_0(u) = g(u)^{1/2}$. Its superpotential is defined with a negative sign conventionally:
$$
W_{\text{susy}} = -\frac{\psi_0'}{\psi_0} = -\frac{1}{2}\frac{g'}{g} = -\frac{1}{2}W_1(u) = \frac{r}{2}\tanh(ru)
$$
This is the $W_2(u)$ operator derived in §15.2. Because of the factor of $1/2$, the square $W_2^2$ and the derivative $W_2'$ no longer cancel their non-constant terms, leading directly to the Pöschl-Teller potential $V_-(u) = r^2/4 - (3r^2/4)\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)$ rather than a free particle.

# 16.5 Fokker-Planck Correspondence

The primary SUSY potential $V_-(u)$ is not exclusively a quantum mechanical construct. It is mathematically identical to the effective Schrödinger potential of a classical stochastic system governed by the Fokker-Planck equation, where the stationary probability distribution is our log-pushed density $p(u;r)$.

$$
\boxed{\; V_{\text{eff}}(u) = \frac{1}{4}(V_{\text{FP}}')^2 - \frac{1}{2}V_{\text{FP}}'' = V_-(u) = \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{3r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \;}
$$

### Exposition
In non-equilibrium statistical mechanics, a diffusion process tending toward a stationary distribution $p(u) \propto e^{-V_{\text{FP}}(u)}$ can be mapped to a Hermitian quantum system. This correspondence reveals that the "quantum ground state energy" $E_0=0$ is actually a statement of thermal equilibrium: the classical probability current has ceased, and the system is perfectly stabilized in the potential landscape.

### Derivation

**Step 1: Identify the Fokker-Planck Potential**
Let the stationary probability distribution be our log-pushed density from §1.3:
$$
p(u;r) = \frac{r}{\pi}\mathrm{sech}(ru)
$$
We define the classical Fokker-Planck drift potential $V_{\text{FP}}(u)$ such that $p(u) \propto e^{-V_{\text{FP}}(u)}$. Therefore:
$$
V_{\text{FP}}(u) = -\log(p(u)) + \text{const} = -\log(\mathrm{sech}(ru)) = \log(\cosh(ru))
$$
*(Note: This is exactly the Maupertuis action $S_0$ identified earlier).*

**Step 2: Map to the Effective Schrödinger Potential**
By standard techniques in stochastic dynamics, the Fokker-Planck equation can be transformed into a self-adjoint Schrödinger-like equation. The effective potential for this mapped equation is given by:
$$
V_{\text{eff}}(u) = \frac{1}{4}(V_{\text{FP}}'(u))^2 - \frac{1}{2}V_{\text{FP}}''(u)
$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the Derivatives**
We calculate the derivatives of the classical potential $V_{\text{FP}}(u) = \log(\cosh(ru))$:
*   $V_{\text{FP}}'(u) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\log(\cosh(ru)) = r\tanh(ru)$
*   $V_{\text{FP}}''(u) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}(r\tanh(ru)) = r^2\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)$

**Step 4: Assemble the Effective Potential**
Substitute these derivatives into the formula from Step 2:
$$
V_{\text{eff}}(u) = \frac{1}{4}\left( r\tanh(ru) \right)^2 - \frac{1}{2}\left( r^2\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \right)
$$
$$
= \frac{r^2}{4}\tanh^2(ru) - \frac{r^2}{2}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
$$
Use the identity $\tanh^2(ru) = 1 - \mathrm{sech}^2(ru)$:
$$
= \frac{r^2}{4}\left( 1 - \mathrm{sech}^2(ru) \right) - \frac{r^2}{2}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
$$
$$
= \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) - \frac{2r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru) = \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{3r^2}{4}\mathrm{sech}^2(ru)
$$

**Step 5: Conclusion**
The resulting effective potential is exactly $V_-(u)$, the primary SUSY potential derived in §16.3. This proves that the quantum mechanical description of the framework is perfectly dual to its classical statistical mechanics description.
