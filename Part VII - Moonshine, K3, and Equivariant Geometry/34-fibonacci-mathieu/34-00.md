$$
\boxed{\; h(m,n) := \mathrm{Im}\bigl((m+ni)^4\bigr) = 4mn(m+n)(m-n) = 4mn(m^2-n^2) \;}
$$

### Exposition
A "congruum" (defined by Fibonacci in 1225 CE) is the difference between areas of nested squares in a rational right triangle problem. In the modern context of Vertex Operator Algebras (VOAs), an $N=4$ superconformal algebra requires four supercharges generated by weight-4 operations on the Ramond lattice.

The congruum $h(m,n)$ operates structurally as the explicit evaluation of this $N=4$ supercharge operator on the left-moving string sector.

### Derivation

**Step 2: Apply the Weight-4 Operator**

$$
q^4 = (m + ni)^4 = m^4 + 4m^3(ni) + 6m^2(ni)^2 + 4m(ni)^3 + (ni)^4
$$

**Step 3: Evaluate the Powers of $i$**
Substitute $i^2 = -1$, $i^3 = -i$, and $i^4 = 1$:
$$
q^4 = m^4 + 4m^3 n i - 6m^2 n^2 - 4m n^3 i + n^4
$$

**Step 4: Separate Real and Imaginary Parts**
Group the real and imaginary components:
*   $\mathrm{Re}(q^4) = m^4 - 6m^2 n^2 + n^4$
*   $\mathrm{Im}(q^4) = 4m^3 n - 4m n^3$

**Step 5: Factor the Imaginary Part**
Factor out the common term $4mn$ from the imaginary component:
$$
\mathrm{Im}(q^4) = 4mn(m^2 - n^2)

$$
\mathrm{Im}(q^4) = 4mn(m+n)(m-n)

This precisely matches the classical definition of the congruum $h(m,n)$, completing the derivation.

In all cases, the congruum is divisible by 8.

**Step 3: Conclusion**
Since $h(m,n)$ is divisible by 3 and by 8, it must be divisible by their least common multiple, $24$.
