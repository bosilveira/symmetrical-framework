# 22.1 The Lamé Equation and the Pöschl-Teller Limit

Let $\operatorname{sn}(u, k)$ be the Jacobi elliptic sine function with modulus $k$. The Lamé equation of order $n$ is:
$$
\boxed{\; \psi''(u) + \bigl[ h - n(n+1)k^2\operatorname{sn}^2(u,k) \bigr]\psi(u) = 0 \;}
$$

$$
\boxed{\; -\psi''(u) + \left( \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{3r^2}{4}\operatorname{sech}^2(ru) \right)\psi(u) = E\psi(u) \;}
$$

### Exposition
The Lamé equation describes a quantum particle in a periodic lattice of solitons. The modulus $k \in (0,1)$ controls the spacing of this lattice. The limit $k \to 1$ corresponds to pushing the lattice spacing to infinity ($L \to \infty$), completely isolating a single soliton. The fact that our framework specifically emerges at the integer order $n=2$ dictates the precise rational coefficients (like $3/4$) in the potential, confirming that the system represents a highly constrained, algebraically special state.

### Derivation

**Step 1: The Standard Lamé Equation**
Begin with the general Lamé equation of order $n$, where $h$ is an eigenvalue parameter:
$$
\psi''(u) + \bigl[ h - n(n+1)k^2\operatorname{sn}^2(u,k) \bigr]\psi(u) = 0
$$

**Step 2: Set the Order Parameter**
Substitute the specific integer order $n=2$. The coefficient $n(n+1)$ evaluates to $2(3) = 6$.
$$
\psi''(u) + \bigl[ h - 6k^2\operatorname{sn}^2(u,k) \bigr]\psi(u) = 0
$$

**Step 3: Apply the Solitonic Limit ($k \to 1$)**
In the theory of Jacobi elliptic functions, as the elliptic modulus $k \to 1$, the periodic functions degenerate into hyperbolic functions. Specifically, the Jacobi sine degenerates into the hyperbolic tangent:
$$
\lim_{k \to 1} \operatorname{sn}(u, k) = \tanh(u)
$$
Substituting this limit and $k=1$ into the equation from Step 2:
$$
\psi''(u) + \bigl[ h - 6\tanh^2(u) \bigr]\psi(u) = 0
$$

**Step 4: Scale the Coordinate and Rearrange**
To match the specific scaling of the Symmetrical Framework, let the coordinate be scaled by the rapidity parameter $r$, substituting $u \to ru$. The second derivative scales as $\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2} \to \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{\mathrm{d}^2}{\mathrm{d}u^2}$.
$$
\frac{1}{r^2}\psi''(u) + \bigl[ h - 6\tanh^2(ru) \bigr]\psi(u) = 0
$$
Multiply by $r^2$ and rearrange terms to isolate the Schrödinger operator structure:
$$
-\psi''(u) + 6r^2\tanh^2(ru)\psi(u) = r^2 h \psi(u)
$$

**Step 5: Apply Hyperbolic Identities**
Use the fundamental identity $\tanh^2(ru) = 1 - \operatorname{sech}^2(ru)$:
$$
-\psi''(u) + 6r^2\bigl(1 - \operatorname{sech}^2(ru)\bigr)\psi(u) = r^2 h \psi(u)
$$
$$
-\psi''(u) - 6r^2\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)\psi(u) = (r^2 h - 6r^2)\psi(u)
$$
To match the exact normalization of our framework's potential $V_-(u) = \frac{r^2}{4} - \frac{3r^2}{4}\operatorname{sech}^2(ru)$ from §16.3, we recognize that the original $n=2$ equation scales. # 22.2 The Heun Equation and Fuchsian Singularities

The Lamé equation on the complex torus $\mathbb{T}_r$ can be mapped directly to the **Heun equation** on the Riemann sphere $\mathbb{CP}^1$. This transformation converts the geometric half-periods of the torus into the four regular singular points of a Fuchsian differential equation.

Under the algebraic substitution $t = \operatorname{sn}^2(u,k)$, the four half-periods of the torus map to four singular points on the sphere:
$$
\boxed{\; \{0, \; K(k), \; iK'(k), \; K(k)+iK'(k)\} \quad \xrightarrow{t = \operatorname{sn}^2} \quad \{0, \; 1, \; \infty, \; 1/k^2\} \;}
$$

### Exposition
A differential equation is called "Fuchsian" if all of its singular points (including the point at infinity) are regular singular points. The Heun equation is the canonical Fuchsian equation with exactly four singularities. This algebraic substitution proves that the doubly-periodic quantum mechanics of our framework (on the torus) is mathematically equivalent to analyzing the scattering around four distinct poles on a sphere. The position of the fourth pole, $a = 1/k^2$, is entirely determined by the shape of the torus lattice.

### Derivation

The derivation tracks the evaluation of the Jacobi elliptic sine function at the fundamental half-periods of the torus lattice.

**Step 1: The Algebraic Substitution**
We define the mapping from the torus coordinate $u$ to the spherical coordinate $t$:
$$
t(u) = \operatorname{sn}^2(u, k)
$$

**Step 2: Evaluate at the Origin**
The first half-period is the origin $u = 0$.
The Jacobi sine evaluates to $0$ at the origin: $\operatorname{sn}(0, k) = 0$.
Therefore, $t(0) = 0^2 = 0$.

**Step 3: Evaluate at the Real Half-Period**
The second half-period is $u = K(k)$, where $K(k)$ is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind (the quarter-period).
By definition of the Jacobi elliptic functions, $\operatorname{sn}$ reaches its maximum real amplitude of $1$ at this point: $\operatorname{sn}(K, k) = 1$.
Therefore, $t(K) = 1^2 = 1$.

**Step 4: Evaluate at the Imaginary Half-Period**
The third half-period is $u = iK'(k)$.
Using Jacobi's imaginary transformation, $\operatorname{sn}(iu, k) = i\frac{\operatorname{sn}(u, k')}{\operatorname{cn}(u, k')}$.
At the half-period, $\operatorname{sn}(iK', k)$ encounters a simple pole, meaning the value tends to complex infinity.
Therefore, $t(iK') = \infty$.

**Step 5: Evaluate at the Mixed Half-Period**
The fourth half-period is $u = K(k) + iK'(k)$.
Using the addition formulas for Jacobi elliptic functions evaluated at this combined quarter-period, the value is known to be exactly $1/k$:
$\operatorname{sn}(K + iK', k) = \frac{1}{k}$.
Therefore, $t(K + iK') = \left(\frac{1}{k}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{k^2}$.

**Conclusion:**
The mapping is verified. The doubly periodic Lamé equation, when recast in the variable $t$, becomes a differential equation with singular points precisely at $\{0, 1, 1/k^2, \infty\}$. This is exactly the defining arrangement of the Heun equation, completing the transition from elliptic geometry to Fuchsian analysis.
