# 21.1 Autonomous Painlevé IV

The canonical conformal phase $W(u) = \tanh(ru)$ generates an exact, algebraic solution to the autonomous reduction of the Painlevé IV (PIV) equation. By scaling the phase function by the rapidity parameter $r$, we obtain a field that satisfies a specific cubic oscillator equation.

Let $w(u) := r W(u) = r \tanh(ru)$. This function satisfies the second-order nonlinear ODE:
$$
\boxed{\; w''(u) = 2w(u)^3 - 2r^2 w(u) \;}
$$

### Exposition
The standard Painlevé IV equation is a non-autonomous second-order ODE of the form $w'' = \frac{(w')^2}{2w} + \frac{3}{2}w^3 + 4zw^2 + 2(z^2-\alpha)w + \frac{\beta}{w}$. However, through specific degenerate parameter choices and coordinate transformations, it reduces to autonomous forms (lacking explicit $u$ dependence). The equation derived here is a classically known autonomous limit of PIV. This demonstrates that the conformal phase of our framework is a fundamental "constant-time" solution to the PIV hierarchy, representing a static topological domain wall (a kink) in the phase space.

### Derivation

The derivation relies purely on the autonomous Riccati equation established in §3.4.

**Step 1: Define the Scaled Variable and its First Derivative**
From §3.4, the conformal phase $W(u)$ satisfies the Riccati equation:
$$
W'(u) = r(1 - W(u)^2)
$$
We define the scaled variable $w(u) = r W(u)$. Substituting $W = w/r$ into the Riccati equation yields:
$$
\frac{1}{r} w'(u) = r \left( 1 - \frac{w(u)^2}{r^2} \right) = r - \frac{w(u)^2}{r}
$$
Multiplying both sides by $r$, we obtain a scaled Bernoulli/Riccati first integral:
$$
w'(u) = r^2 - w(u)^2
$$

**Step 2: Differentiate to Obtain the Second-Order ODE**
To find the equation governing the second derivative, we differentiate the first integral with respect to $u$:
$$
w''(u) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} \left( r^2 - w(u)^2 \right) = -2w(u) w'(u)
$$

**Step 3: Substitute and Simplify**
Substitute the expression for $w'(u)$ from Step 1 back into the equation from Step 2:
$$
w''(u) = -2w(u) \left( r^2 - w(u)^2 \right) = -2r^2 w(u) + 2w(u)^3
$$
Rearranging the terms yields the final boxed identity:
$$
w''(u) = 2w(u)^3 - 2r^2 w(u)
$$
This completes the derivation, structurally linking the Riccati geometry to the autonomous PIV hierarchy.

# 21.2 Autonomous Painlevé II

Just as the conformal phase connects to Painlevé IV, the Rosetta Stone core object $S(u) = \operatorname{sech}(ru)$ generates an exact solution to the autonomous reduction of the Painlevé II (PII) equation.

Let $v(u) := r S(u) = r \operatorname{sech}(ru)$. This function satisfies the second-order nonlinear ODE:
$$
\boxed{\; v''(u) = r^2 v(u) - 2v(u)^3 \;}
$$

### Exposition
The standard Painlevé II equation is $v'' = 2v^3 + zu + \alpha$. By removing the explicit coordinate dependence ($z=0$) and setting the constant $\alpha=0$, one obtains the autonomous PII equation. The identity above proves that the scaled half-density of our framework is the exact solitary wave solution to this autonomous limit. This is mathematically equivalent to the stationary modified Korteweg-de Vries (mKdV) soliton equation, reinforcing the deep intersection between the Painlevé transcendents and soliton PDEs.

### Derivation

The derivation utilizes the coupled first-order system of derivatives for $S$ and $W$ established in §3.1.

**Step 1: Define the Scaled Variable and its First Derivative**
Let $v(u) = r S(u)$.
From §3.1, the derivative of $S(u)$ is $S'(u) = -r S(u) W(u)$. Therefore, the derivative of $v(u)$ is:
$$
v'(u) = r S'(u) = -r^2 S(u) W(u) = -r v(u) W(u)
$$

**Step 2: Differentiate to Obtain the Second-Order ODE**
We differentiate $v'(u)$ with respect to $u$ using the product rule:
$$
v''(u) = \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} \left( -r^2 S(u) W(u) \right) = -r^2 \left[ S'(u)W(u) + S(u)W'(u) \right]
$$

**Step 3: Substitute First-Order Relations**
From §3.1, we substitute $S'(u) = -r S(u) W(u)$ and $W'(u) = r S(u)^2$:
$$
v''(u) = -r^2 \left[ (-r S(u) W(u)) W(u) + S(u) (r S(u)^2) \right]
$$
$$
= -r^2 \left[ -r S(u) W(u)^2 + r S(u)^3 \right] = r^3 S(u) W(u)^2 - r^3 S(u)^3
$$

**Step 4: Apply the Half-Density Law**
Use the fundamental identity $W^2 = 1 - S^2$ to eliminate $W$:
$$
v''(u) = r^3 S(u) (1 - S(u)^2) - r^3 S(u)^3 = r^3 S(u) - r^3 S(u)^3 - r^3 S(u)^3
$$
$$
= r^3 S(u) - 2r^3 S(u)^3
$$

**Step 5: Translate Back to the Scaled Variable $v$**
Since $v = rS$, we have $v^3 = r^3 S^3$. Substituting these back yields:
$$
v''(u) = r^2 (rS(u)) - 2(r^3S(u)^3) = r^2 v(u) - 2v(u)^3
$$
This yields the final boxed identity, completing the derivation.

# 21.3 Confluence Limit: PIV to PII

The Painlevé equations form a rigid degeneration cascade, where higher-order transcendents collapse into lower-order ones under specific "confluence" limits. $$
\boxed{\; w'' = 2w^3 - 2r^2w \quad \xrightarrow{\text{deformation}} \quad q_{zz} = 2q^3 + z q \;}
$$

### Exposition
In the autonomous regime (constant $r$), the phase $W(u)$ defines a static domain wall. However, if we break the scale invariance of the system by forcing the parameter $r^2$ to depend linearly on the spatial coordinate $u$ (a symmetry-breaking deformation), the system is thrust out of the autonomous limit. The result is the emergence of the true Hastings-McLeod solution of the standard Painlevé II equation. This formally proves that PII is the non-autonomous, symmetry-broken shadow of our framework's PIV structure.

### Derivation

The derivation requires an algebraic substitution and a subsequent rescaling of the coordinates to match the canonical PII form.

**Step 1: The Initial Autonomous Equation**
From §21.1, we have the autonomous PIV equation:
$$
w_{uu} = 2w^3 - 2r^2w
$$

**Step 2: Apply the Non-Autonomous Deformation**
We break the scale invariance by replacing the constant $r^2$ with a function of the coordinate. We choose the specific linear deformation $r^2 \to -u/2$. Substituting this into the ODE gives a non-autonomous equation:
$$
w_{uu} = 2w^3 - 2\left(-\frac{u}{2}\right)w = 2w^3 + u w
$$

**Step 3: Rescale the Variables**
To reach the standard canonical form of PII, we introduce scaled variables. Let $w = \alpha q$ and $u = \beta z$, where $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are constants to be determined.
The derivatives transform as $\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u} = \frac{1}{\beta}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}$, which means $w_{uu} = \frac{\alpha}{\beta^2} q_{zz}$.
Substitute these into the deformed equation:
$$
\frac{\alpha}{\beta^2} q_{zz} = 2(\alpha^3 q^3) + (\beta z)(\alpha q)
$$
Divide the entire equation by $\alpha / \beta^2$:
$$
q_{zz} = 2\alpha^2\beta^2 q^3 + \beta^3 z q
$$

**Step 4: Solve for the Scaling Constants**
For this to match the standard PII equation $q_{zz} = 2q^3 + zq$, we must enforce:
1.  $2\alpha^2\beta^2 = 2 \implies \alpha\beta = 1$
2.  $\beta^3 = 1 \implies \beta = 1$

Therefore, $\beta = 1$ and $\alpha = 1$. The deformation $r^2 \to -u/2$ mapped the system *directly* into the canonical PII equation without requiring further scaling. (If a different linear term like $r^2 \to -u$ were used, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ would adapt via cube roots of 2).

This confirms the structural deformation from the autonomous PIV of the Symmetrical Framework to the standard non-autonomous PII.

# 21.4 Double-Scaling Limit: Sech to Airy

\boxed{\; -\frac{\mathrm{d}^2\psi}{\mathrm{d}T^2} + T^2 \psi = 0 \quad \text{(The Airy Equation for a Parabolic Well)} \;}
$$

### Exposition
This limit represents the physical transition from a deep, non-linear solitonic well (the Pöschl-Teller potential) to a simple harmonic oscillator well.
