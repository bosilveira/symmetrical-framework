# 19.2 The Wronskian Structure

### Exposition

The Wronskian formulation is the bridge between linear differential equations and non-linear integrable PDEs. ### Derivation

The derivation utilizes standard linear algebra transformations to map the Cauchy determinant into a Wronskian.

**Step 1: The Wronskian Definition**
The Wronskian of the functions $\phi_1, \dots, \phi_N$ is the determinant of the $N \times N$ matrix $M$ where $M_{ij} = \partial_\xi^{i-1} \phi_j$.
For $\phi_j(\xi) = e^{r_j \xi} + e^{-r_j \xi}$ (which is $2\cosh(r_j \xi)$), the derivatives generate alternating powers of the rapidities $r_j$.

**Step 2: Relate Wronskian to the Vandermonde Matrix**
By factoring out the exponential terms from the columns of the Wronskian matrix, the remaining matrix resembles a Vandermonde matrix defined by the rapidities $r_j$.
The classic property of the Wronskian of pure exponentials $e^{r_j \xi}$ is that $\operatorname{Wr}(e^{r_1 \xi}, \dots, e^{r_N \xi}) = \Delta(r_1, \dots, r_N) \exp(\sum r_j \xi)$, where $\Delta$ is the Vandermonde determinant $\prod_{i<j}(r_j - r_i)$.

**Step 3: Equivalence to the Cauchy Determinant**
From §18.4, we established that our tau function $\tau_N = \det[I + 2DCD]$ relies on the Cauchy determinant $\det C = \frac{\prod_{i<j}(r_i - r_j)^2}{\prod_{i,j}(r_i + r_j)}$.

**Step 4: Gauge Invariance of the Physical Field**

$$

Thus, the symmetrical framework tau function and the pure Wronskian are physically equivalent representations of the exact same $N$-soliton manifold.

# 19.3 Plücker Relations and the Desnanot-Jacobi Identity

$$
\boxed{\; \det(M) \cdot \det(M_{12,12}) = \det(M_{1,1}) \cdot \det(M_{2,2}) - \det(M_{1,2}) \cdot \det(M_{2,1}) \;}
$$
*(where $M_{i,j}$ denotes the submatrix formed by deleting row $i$ and column $j$).*

### Exposition
In the coordinate ring of the Grassmannian, the Plücker relations are the fundamental quadratic constraints that define which points actually lie on the manifold. When the Sato tau function is expressed as a determinant, these geometric constraints translate algebraically into the Desnanot-Jacobi identity. Taking the continuous limit of this discrete matrix identity natively generates the Hirota $D$-operator differential equations. This establishes that **integrable PDEs are just the continuous shadow of the geometry of the Grassmannian**.

### Derivation

We demonstrate how the discrete tau function recurrence follows from this matrix identity.

**Step 1: Assign the Tau Functions to Submatrices**
Let $M$ be the $(N) \times (N)$ block matrix defining the $N$-soliton tau function $\tau_N$. 
We define the sequence of tau functions by taking principal submatrices:
*   $\det(M) = \tau_N$
*   $\det(M_{1,1}) = \tau_{N-1}^+$ (The $(N-1)$-soliton tau excluding the first mode)
*   $\det(M_{2,2}) = \tau_{N-1}^-$ (The $(N-1)$-soliton tau excluding the second mode)
*   $\det(M_{12,12}) = \tau_{N-2}$ (The $(N-2)$-soliton tau excluding both)

**Step 2: Apply the Desnanot-Jacobi Identity**
Substituting these identifications into the boxed identity yields a recurrence relation:
$$
\tau_N \cdot \tau_{N-2} = \tau_{N-1}^+ \tau_{N-1}^- - \det(M_{1,2})\det(M_{2,1})

**Step 3: The Wronskian Plücker Form**
When applied specifically to the Wronskian representation of the tau function ($\tau = \det(\phi_1, \phi_2, \dots, \phi_N)$), the Desnanot-Jacobi identity takes the specific derivative form known as the Wronskian Plücker relation for $3 \times 3$ systems:

\det(\phi_1, \phi_2, \phi_3) = \det(\phi_1, \phi_2)\phi_3' - \det(\phi_1, \phi_3)\phi_2' + \det(\phi_2, \phi_3)\phi_1'

**Step 4: Transition to Hirota Bilinear Equations**
