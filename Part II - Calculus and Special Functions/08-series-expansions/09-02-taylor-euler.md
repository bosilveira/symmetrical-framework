# 9.2 Local Taylor Series (Euler Numbers)

To describe the function's local geometry around its center of symmetry, $x=c$, we can expand it as a Taylor series in the canonical logarithmic coordinate. This expansion is generated by the classical **Euler numbers**.

For $x$ in the vicinity of $c$, the function can be expressed as:

$$
\boxed{ f(x;r,c) = \frac{1}{2x} \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{E_{2n}}{(2n)!} \left(r\log\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)\right)^{2n} }
$$

where $E_{2n}$ are the Euler numbers ($E_0=1, E_2=-1, E_4=5, \dots$). The series converges for:

$$
\left|\log\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)\right| < \frac{\pi}{2r} \quad \iff \quad c e^{-\pi/(2r)} < x < c e^{\pi/(2r)}
$$

### Exposition

This local series provides the exact behavior of the function near its central point. Unlike the geometric series from §9.1, which converge away from the center, this series converges in a symmetric interval *around* the center. The appearance of the Euler numbers here explicitly connects the function's local geometry (e.g., its curvature) to the same combinatorial sequence that governs its logarithmic moments (§7.3), demonstrating a deep analytic consistency.

### Derivation

The derivation is a direct application of the Rosetta Stone identity, translating the known Taylor series of the hyperbolic secant function into the framework's notation.

**Step 1: Invoke the Rosetta Stone Identity**

From §1.2, we have the fundamental isomorphism:

$$
f(x;r,c) = \frac{1}{2x} \mathrm{sech}\left(r\log\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)\right)
$$

Let the canonical logarithmic coordinate be $u = r\log(x/c)$, so that $2x f(x) = \mathrm{sech}(u)$.

**Step 2: Apply the Maclaurin Series for Hyperbolic Secant**

The classical Maclaurin series expansion for the hyperbolic secant function around $u=0$ is generated by the even-indexed Euler numbers:

$$
\mathrm{sech}(u) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{E_{2n}}{(2n)!} u^{2n}
$$

The odd Euler numbers are all zero, reflecting the fact that $\mathrm{sech}(u)$ is an even function.

**Step 3: Substitute and Rearrange**

Substitute $u = r\log(x/c)$ back into the series:

$$
2x f(x;r,c) = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{E_{2n}}{(2n)!} \left(r\log\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)\right)^{2n}
$$

Dividing both sides by $2x$ immediately yields the boxed formula.

**Step 4: Determine the Radius of Convergence**

The radius of convergence for a Taylor series is determined by the distance from the expansion point to the nearest singularity of the function in the complex plane.

*   The function $\mathrm{sech}(u) = 1/\cosh(u)$ has poles where $\cosh(u) = 0$.
*   The roots of $\cosh(u)$ occur at purely imaginary values: $u = \pm i\frac{\pi}{2}, \pm i\frac{3\pi}{2}, \dots$
*   The distance from the origin $u=0$ to the nearest poles is exactly $|u| = \pi/2$.

Therefore, the series converges strictly for $|u| < \pi/2$. Substituting our definition of $u$:

$$
\left|r\log\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)\right| < \frac{\pi}{2} \quad \implies \quad \left|\log\left(\frac{x}{c}\right)\right| < \frac{\pi}{2r}
$$

This defines the interval of convergence for $x$.
