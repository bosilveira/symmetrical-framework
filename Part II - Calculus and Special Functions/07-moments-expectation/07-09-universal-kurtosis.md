# 7.9 Log-Cumulants and Universal Kurtosis

The cumulants of a probability distribution provide a sequence of descriptive constants that are often more mathematically tractable and physically meaningful than raw moments. For this framework, the cumulants of the logarithmic variable $U = \log(X/c)$ are directly proportional to the **Tangent numbers** and reveal a universal, parameter-free value for the kurtosis.

### Cumulant Generating Function (CGF)

The CGF, $K(t) = \log(\mathbb{E}[e^{tU}])$, is the logarithm of the moment generating function from §7.1:

$$
\boxed{ K(t) = \log\left(\sec\left(\frac{\pi t}{2r}\right)\right) }
$$

### Log-Cumulants

Since the CGF is an even function, all odd-order cumulants ($\kappa_1, \kappa_3, \dots$) are zero. The even-order cumulants are given by:

$$
\boxed{ \kappa_{2n} = T_{n}\left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n} }
$$

where $T_n$ are the Tangent numbers ($T_1=1, T_2=2, T_3=16, \dots$).

### Key Statistical Metrics

*   **Mean:** $\kappa_1 = \mathbb{E}[U] = 0$.
*   **Variance:** $\text{Var}(U) = \kappa_2 = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$.
*   **Skewness:** $\gamma_1 = \kappa_3 / \kappa_2^{3/2} = 0$.
*   **Excess Kurtosis:** $\gamma_2 = \kappa_4 / \kappa_2^2 = 2$.
*   **Pearson Kurtosis:** $\beta_2 = \gamma_2 + 3 = 5$.

### Exposition

This result connects the statistical shape of the logarithmic distribution directly to classical combinatorial numbers. The zero skewness confirms the distribution's symmetry. Most remarkably, the excess kurtosis evaluates to a universal constant, `2`, completely independent of both the shape parameter `r` and the scale parameter `c`. This means that while `r` controls the spread (variance), the fundamental "shape" of the tails in logarithmic space is an unchangeable invariant of the entire family. A Pearson kurtosis of 5 indicates the distribution is strictly **leptokurtic** (more peaked and with heavier tails than a Gaussian distribution).

### Derivation

**Step 1:** The cumulants $\kappa_n$ are the coefficients of the Maclaurin series of the CGF: $K(t) = \sum \kappa_n t^n/n!$.

**Step 2:** The classical Maclaurin series for $\log\sec(z)$ is generated by the Tangent numbers: $\log\sec(z) = \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{T_n}{(2n)!} z^{2n}$.

**Step 3:** Substitute $z = \pi t/(2r)$ into the series and match coefficients with the definition of the CGF to extract the cumulants $\kappa_{2n}$.

**Step 4:** The kurtosis is computed from the ratio $\kappa_4/\kappa_2^2 = \frac{T_2(\pi/2r)^4}{(T_1(\pi/2r)^2)^2} = T_2/T_1^2 = 2/1^2 = 2$.
