# 11.3 Large-x Asymptotic Behavior

When the Stieltjes transform is evaluated far from the origin on the negative real axis (as $x \to \infty$), it exhibits a simple, universal inverse-distance decay. The coefficient of this decay is governed entirely by the total integrated mass of the function.

As $x \to \infty$:

$$
\boxed{ S_{f}(-x;r,c) \sim \frac{\pi}{2rx} }
$$

### Exposition

This asymptotic relationship is a fundamental property of Stieltjes transforms. In physical contexts where the transform represents a potential generated by a mass or charge distribution, this result states that from a great distance, the extended distribution $f(t)$ has the same leading-order effect as a single point mass located at the origin with a total mass equal to $\int_0^\infty f(t) \mathrm{d}t$. The derivation makes this connection precise.

### Derivation

The derivation relies on factoring the integrand and applying the Dominated Convergence Theorem to justify passing the limit inside the integral sign.

**Step 1: Factor the Integral**

Start with the definition of the Stieltjes transform at $z = -x$:

$$
S_{f}(-x) = \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(t;r,c)}{t+x}\,\mathrm{d}t
$$

Factor out the term $1/x$ from the denominator of the integrand:

$$
S_{f}(-x) = \frac{1}{x} \int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{f(t;r,c)}{1 + t/x}\,\mathrm{d}t
$$

**Step 2: Evaluate the Asymptotic Limit**

We aim to determine the limit of the integral as $x \to \infty$. To do this formally, we examine the limit of the product $x S_f(-x)$.

$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} \left[ x S_f(-x) \right] = \lim_{x \to \infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{f(t;r,c)}{1 + t/x}\,\mathrm{d}t
$$

For any fixed integration point $t > 0$, the kernel of the integrand has the limit:

$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} \frac{1}{1 + t/x} = \frac{1}{1+0} = 1
$$

**Step 3: Apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem**

To justify moving the limit inside the integral, we need a dominating function. For all $x>0$ and $t>0$, the kernel is bounded: $0 < \frac{1}{1+t/x} \le 1$.

Therefore, the integrand is bounded by the function itself:

$$
0 < \frac{f(t;r,c)}{1 + t/x} \le f(t;r,c)
$$

From §5.3, we know that $f(t;r,c)$ is integrable on $(0, \infty)$, with $\int_0^\infty f(t) \mathrm{d}t = \pi/(2r)$.

Since the integrand is pointwise convergent and bounded by an integrable function, the Dominated Convergence Theorem applies. We can exchange the limit and the integral:

$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{f(t;r,c)}{1 + t/x}\,\mathrm{d}t = \int_{0}^{\infty} \lim_{x \to \infty} \left( \frac{f(t;r,c)}{1 + t/x} \right) \mathrm{d}t = \int_{0}^{\infty} f(t;r,c) \,\mathrm{d}t
$$

**Step 4: State the Final Asymptotic Relation**

From §5.3, the total mass integral is $\frac{\pi}{2r}$. Therefore:

$$
\lim_{x \to \infty} \left[ x S_f(-x) \right] = \frac{\pi}{2r}
$$

This is the definition of the asymptotic relationship:

$$
S_{f}(-x) \sim \frac{\pi}{2rx} \quad \text{as } x \to \infty
$$

This completes the derivation.
