# Chapter 4 — Geometric properties: corrected graph, lengths, and curvature

**Mathematical revision, 20 November 2026.** The central point $`x=c`$ is the inversion center, not generally the maximum of the graph. The shape changes are controlled by two distinct thresholds: $`r=1`$ for the existence of an interior maximum, and $`r=\sqrt{7/8}`$ plus $`r=1,2`$ for the number of genuine inflection points.

When discussing a Euclidean graph of $`f`$, specify units: $`f`$ has inverse-length units if $`x,c`$ are lengths. A dimensionally homogeneous graph uses $`(x,Y)=(x,c^2f(x))`$, equivalently dimensionless coordinates $`(t,k_r(t))`$ with $`t=x/c`$, $`k_r(t)=cf(ct)`$. The historical numerical arc-length formulas for the graph $`(x,f(x))`$ are still mathematically well-defined in **chosen numerical units**, but are not invariant under changing length units.

**Key corrections:** the number and polynomial of inflection points are not as claimed previously; the $`r=1`$ graph-length differential naturally lives on a genus-two algebraic curve, not automatically a genus-one elliptic curve; substituting lemniscatic coordinates at $`r=2`$ does not itself establish elliptic reducibility. For the surface formed by revolving the raw numerical graph, volume is finite exactly for $`r>1/2`$, while surface area is finite exactly for $`r\ge1`$ (the earlier strict $`r>1`$ bound erroneously excluded $`r=1`$).
