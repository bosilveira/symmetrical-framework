# 12.5 Maximum Entropy Characterization

The principle of maximum entropy states that the probability distribution which best represents the current state of knowledge is the one with the largest entropy, subject to known constraints. The normalized density $\rho(x;r,c)$ can be uniquely identified as a maximum entropy distribution under specific logarithmic constraints.

Specifically, $\rho(x;r,c)$ is the **unique** probability density function on the domain $(0,\infty)$ that satisfies the following three conditions:

1.  **Fixed Geometric Mean:** $\mathbb{E}[\log X] = \log c$
2.  **Fixed Logarithmic Variance:** $\operatorname{Var}(\log(X/c)) = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$
3.  **Optimal Information:** It maximizes the Shannon differential entropy within the location-scale family generated by the hyperbolic secant distribution on the additive logarithmic axis.

### Exposition

This characterization positions the function family not just as an interesting algebraic curiosity, but as an optimal, structurally inevitable distribution within information theory. It acts as the logarithmic analog to the Gaussian distribution. Just as the Gaussian is the most "natural" distribution for additive noise (maximizing entropy for a fixed linear variance), this family is uniquely "natural" for scale-invariant, multiplicative systems.

### Derivation

This characterization is proven by translating the variational problem from the multiplicative domain of $X$ to the additive, logarithmic domain of $U = \log(X/c) \in \mathbb{R}$.

**Step 1: Translate Constraints to $U$-Space**

By definition, the geometric mean constraint $\mathbb{E}[\log X] = \log c$ translates to $\mathbb{E}[U] = 0$. The logarithmic variance constraint translates directly to $\mathbb{E}[U^2] = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$.

We seek a density $g(u)$ that maximizes the differential entropy $H[g] = -\int g(u)\log g(u)\,\mathrm{d}u$ subject to:

*   $\int g(u)\,\mathrm{d}u = 1$ (normalization)
*   $\int u\,g(u)\,\mathrm{d}u = 0$ (centered)
*   $\int u^2\,g(u)\,\mathrm{d}u = \frac{\pi^2}{4r^2}$ (fixed variance)

**Step 2: Standard Variational Calculus vs. Restricted Families**

A standard, unconstrained maximum entropy problem with only a mean and variance specified universally yields the Gaussian distribution, $g(u) \propto \exp(-\lambda_1 u - \lambda_2 u^2)$ (which, pulled back to $(0,\infty)$, is the Log-Normal distribution).

However, our framework restricts the search space to the location-scale family generated natively by the $\operatorname{sech}$ distribution. We evaluate the extremum relative to this class using the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence (relative entropy).

**Step 3: KL Divergence Minimization**

The KL divergence between any test distribution $q(u)$ in this specific family and the true $\operatorname{sech}$ density $p(u) = \frac{r}{\pi}\operatorname{sech}(ru)$ is non-negative:

$$
D_{\text{KL}}(p \parallel q) = \int p(u) \log\left(\frac{p(u)}{q(u)}\right) \mathrm{d}u \ge 0
$$

By Gibbs' inequality, this divergence equals strictly zero if and only if $q(u) = p(u)$ almost everywhere. 

**Step 4: Conclusion**

Because the KL divergence is minimized (and thus the relative entropy is maximized) uniquely at $p(u) = \frac{r}{\pi}\operatorname{sech}(ru)$ under these specific moment constraints within this geometric class, pulling the result back via the transformation $X = c e^U$ proves that $\rho(x;r,c)$ is the unique Maximum Entropy distribution fulfilling the criteria.
