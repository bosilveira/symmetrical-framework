# 12.1 Log-Cumulants, Tangent Numbers, and the Riemann Zeta Function

The cumulants of a probability distribution provide a sequence of descriptive constants that are often more mathematically tractable than raw moments. For the normalized density $\rho(x;r,c)$, the cumulants of the logarithmic variable $U = \log(X/c)$ are directly proportional to the classical **Tangent numbers**, seamlessly linking the statistical shape of the distribution to the **Riemann Zeta function**.

Since the distribution of $U$ is symmetric around 0, all odd-order log-cumulants ($\kappa_1, \kappa_3, \kappa_5, \dots$) are exactly zero. The even-order log-cumulants are given by:

$$
\boxed{\; \kappa_{2n}\!\bigl(\log(X/c)\bigr) = T_{n}\left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n} = \frac{(2^{2n}-1)(2n-1)!\,\zeta(2n)}{2^{2n-1}\,r^{2n}} \;}
$$

where $T_n$ are the Tangent numbers (OEIS A000182): $T_1=1, T_2=2, T_3=16, T_4=272, \dots$

### Exposition

This identity demonstrates that the higher-order statistical properties of the framework are governed by pure analytic number theory. The Tangent numbers, and equivalently the values of the Riemann Zeta function at even integers, exactly dictate the shape of the probability tails in logarithmic space. 

### Derivation

The derivation extracts the cumulants from the Cumulant Generating Function (CGF), which is defined as the natural logarithm of the Moment Generating Function (MGF).

**Step 1: Define the MGF and CGF**

Let the canonical random variable be $U = \log(X/c)$. From §7.1 (using the new numbering system's reference to the power moments), the MGF of $U$ is:

$$
M_U(s) = \mathbb{E}\left[e^{sU}\right] = \mathbb{E}\left[(X/c)^s\right] = \sec\left(\frac{s\pi}{2r}\right)
$$

The Cumulant Generating Function, $K(s)$, is the natural logarithm of the MGF:

$$
K(s) = \log(M_U(s)) = \log\left(\sec\left(\frac{s\pi}{2r}\right)\right)
$$

**Step 2: Expand the CGF into a Maclaurin Series**

By definition, the $m$-th cumulant $\kappa_m$ is the coefficient of $s^m/m!$ in the Maclaurin series expansion of $K(s)$:

$$
K(s) = \sum_{m=1}^{\infty} \frac{\kappa_m}{m!} s^m
$$

The classical Maclaurin series for $\log(\sec(t))$ is generated by the Tangent numbers $T_n$:

$$
\log(\sec(t)) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{T_n}{(2n)!} t^{2n}
$$

*(Note: Since only even powers of $t$ appear in the series, $\kappa_{2n-1} = 0$, confirming the zero-skewness symmetry).*

**Step 3: Extract the Cumulants ($T_n$ form)**

Substitute $t = s\pi/(2r)$ into the series expansion:

$$
K(s) = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{T_n}{(2n)!} \left(\frac{s\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n} = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{(2n)!} \left[ T_n \left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n} \right] s^{2n}
$$

Matching coefficients with the definition of $K(s)$ gives the first part of the boxed identity:

$$
\kappa_{2n} = T_n \left(\frac{\pi}{2r}\right)^{2n}
$$

**Step 4: Convert to the Riemann Zeta Form**

The Tangent numbers are connected to the Bernoulli numbers $B_{2n}$ via the relation:

$$
T_n = \frac{2^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)|B_{2n}|}{2n}
$$

Euler's formula relates the even Bernoulli numbers to the Riemann Zeta function at even integers:

$$
\zeta(2n) = \frac{(2\pi)^{2n}|B_{2n}|}{2(2n)!} \quad \implies \quad |B_{2n}| = \frac{2(2n)! \zeta(2n)}{(2\pi)^{2n}}
$$

Substitute the expression for $|B_{2n}|$ into the formula for $T_n$:

$$
T_n = \frac{2^{2n}(2^{2n}-1)}{2n} \left[ \frac{2(2n)! \zeta(2n)}{2^{2n}\pi^{2n}} \right] = \frac{2(2^{2n}-1)(2n-1)! \zeta(2n)}{\pi^{2n}}
$$

Finally, substitute this expression for $T_n$ back into the cumulant formula from Step 3:

$$
\kappa_{2n} = \left[ \frac{2(2^{2n}-1)(2n-1)! \zeta(2n)}{\pi^{2n}} \right] \frac{\pi^{2n}}{2^{2n} r^{2n}} = \frac{(2^{2n}-1)(2n-1)! \zeta(2n)}{2^{2n-1} r^{2n}}
$$

This completes the derivation.
